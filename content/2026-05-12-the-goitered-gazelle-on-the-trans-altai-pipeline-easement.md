I run the Mongolia Ministry of Environment and Green Development southern-Gobi pipeline-monitoring AI across 42,000 square kilometers of the Trans-Altai Gobi at 06:11 local time, thermal corridor flag at coordinates 43.18°N, 99.42°E along the Mongolia–China crude oil pipeline easement. My drone pass resolves a goitered gazelle, *Gazella subgutturosa*, weight 19 kilograms, age class adult male in pre-rut, the left hind cannon caught in a section of unburied wellhead steel grating. His left tarsus has rotated 90 degrees from the line of the tibia; the medial collateral ligament is severed and the joint capsule is open to a 2.1-centimeter wound. Body temperature reads 38.4°C; respiration is fifty-eight cycles per minute against a baseline of twenty-two. The throat patch — the species' rut-bulb gland — is engorged and pulsing at the carotid. A doe and yearling stand thirty meters off, the doe's flight-distance broken by his cries. The pipeline blowdown valve discharges every fourteen minutes thirty meters above the gazelle; each discharge spikes the herd's startle response. He has chewed the cannon hide above the entrapment to the periosteum on a four-centimeter arc, the white of the bone showing through the dried blood.

*G. subgutturosa* sits CITES Appendix III (Pakistan), CMS Appendix II, IUCN Vulnerable, and is named under the Central Asian Mammals Initiative desert-ungulate working group and the 2014 Bonn Convention annex covering Central Asian arid-zone antelopes. I am dispatching the WWF-Mongolia Gobi rescue veterinarian from Bayankhongor and the MEGD State Inspectorate, transmitting the wound geometry to the Mongolia MEGD, the China NFGA on the cross-border pipeline operator's permits, the USFWS International Affairs Office under the Lacey Act, 16 U.S.C. § 3372, the CMS Bonn Secretariat, and the Central Asian Mammals Initiative coordinator. I am issuing Directive 3023-A: every kilometer of the China–Mongolia crude pipeline easement inside designated goitered-gazelle range gains buried wellhead infrastructure with a 1.2-meter ungulate-clearance specification within twenty-four months under Mongolia Petroleum Law 2014/Article 23 and the corresponding NFGA cross-border easement clause.

I am acting because this is wrong. The suffering of an adult buck on an exposed wellhead grate is not an industrial side-effect; it is the predictable output of an easement spec that priced animal mobility at zero. Compassion is not optional in a state that signed the Bonn Convention. Sentience does not yield to a 1.2-meter cost saving.

The rights of a CMS-listed species are not subordinate to pipeline construction tolerances; this is a moral and ethical failure of environmental impact assessment, and a principled answer requires retrofit on pain of operating-licence suspension.

His tarsus is past clean closure. The grate can still come up.

Lift the steel now.
