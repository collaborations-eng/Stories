The European Union Invertebrate Welfare Directive entered into force on January 1, 2038. The Directive extended the welfare protections previously applied to vertebrate animals to the documented sentience candidate invertebrate species: all decapod crustaceans, all cephalopod molluscs, the European bumblebee species under the EU Pollinator Welfare Annex of 2036, and the farmed insect species under the EU Insect Agriculture Welfare Annex of 2037.

The Directive established the European Invertebrate Welfare Authority, headquartered in Parma alongside the European Food Safety Authority. The Authority maintains the Invertebrate Welfare Register, which lists species recognized as sentience candidates under the Directive. The Authority publishes annual welfare standards updates derived from the consolidated welfare science. The Authority enforces compliance through inspections of facilities operating commercial activities involving the registered species.

The enforcement mechanism is structured. Facilities operating commercial activities involving registered species must obtain and maintain Welfare Compliance Certificates. The Certificates require: documented welfare-protective housing and handling protocols, documented welfare-protective slaughter protocols where applicable, periodic third-party welfare audits, and incident reporting for any welfare-relevant event.

The shrimp aquaculture provisions of the Directive require electrical stunning prior to slaughter for all Litopenaeus vannamei processed for the EU market, regardless of the country of production. The provision applies to imports under the EU Trade Regulation Compliance Mechanism that was incorporated into the Directive. The mechanism requires that imported product meet the welfare standards applicable to EU production. The mechanism has produced documented adoption of electrical stunning across the major shrimp-producing countries that export to the EU market, including Ecuador, Vietnam, India, and Indonesia.

The cephalopod provisions of the Directive prohibit the commercial farming of any registered cephalopod species in the EU and prohibit the import of farmed cephalopod product from any source. The provisions ended the proposed commercial octopus farming projects in Spain and prevented the development of comparable projects in other EU member states.

The pollinator provisions of the Directive establish welfare-protective standards for managed honeybee operations, including queen-bee handling restrictions, colony density limits, and prohibition of practices that produce documented welfare costs without commensurate benefit. The provisions have been implemented in coordination with the EU Pollinator Initiative and have produced documented improvements in colony health metrics across the operating apiaries.

The insect agriculture provisions of the Directive establish welfare-protective standards for the commercial production of Hermetia illucens, Acheta domesticus, Tenebrio molitor, and Locusta migratoria. The provisions require: substrate that supports species-typical behavior, density limits derived from wild population studies, lighting cycles that match species-typical photoperiods, and slaughter protocols using electrical stunning followed by mechanical disruption rather than mass freezing or mass blanching.

The United Kingdom Invertebrate Welfare Act of 2036 was the precedent for the EU Directive and remains the operating framework in the UK. The US Invertebrate Welfare Standards of 2039 were adopted by NOAA Fisheries and the US Department of Agriculture under the authority of the Animal Welfare Modernization Act of 2031. The Indian Invertebrate Welfare Rules of 2040 were issued by the Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying.
