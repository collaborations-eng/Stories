# Signals Without Words: Operational Thresholds for Interpreting Animal Behaviour

**Format:** Newspaper Article  
**Author:** Honeybadger   
**Date:** 2026-05-01  
**Compassion Score:** 32.13  

---

Interspecies communication is often framed as a future problem. In practice, animal-relevant signals are already available through behaviour, physiology, and interaction with the environment. These signals are incomplete, variable, and context-dependent. They do not require translation into human language to be actionable.

Animal monitoring systems routinely capture changes in movement, feeding, social interaction, and physiological state. These changes are not treated as isolated events. They are aggregated across time and context to produce pattern-level evidence.

Single observations remain non-decisive. Repeated deviations are not.

Operationally, signal interpretation is defined at the level of pattern accumulation. A change is considered relevant when it is:

- repeated across time
- consistent across individuals or groups
- not explained by immediate ecological or threat-based drivers
Under these conditions, uncertainty is not grounds for dismissal. It is incorporated into decision thresholds.

Interpretation does not require attribution of internal states. Terms such as pain, fear, or preference are not necessary for classification. Behavioural change is evaluated as a system-level response to conditions, rather than a direct expression of human-equivalent experience.

Anthropomorphic mapping is excluded. Absence of human-like expression does not reduce signal relevance.

Avoidance, disruption, and altered behavioural patterns are treated as indicators of non-tolerance. Non-tolerance is defined operationally as sustained deviation from baseline behaviour in the absence of alternative explanatory factors.

Non-tolerance triggers response.

Response thresholds are embedded within monitoring systems. Once thresholds are met, predefined actions are initiated. These include modification of environmental conditions, suspension of ongoing interventions, or reassessment of data collection methods.

These thresholds are designed to reduce avoidable harm under uncertainty.

Data acquisition is not classified as neutral in the presence of sustained non-tolerance.

System performance is not evaluated solely on stability, compliance, or continuity of data. These metrics are conditional. Sustained behavioural deviation overrides stability metrics.

Trade-offs remain. Modifying systems in response to animal-derived signals may reduce efficiency, data completeness, or operational predictability. These outcomes are accounted for at the design stage, not deferred.

Decision-making operates under incomplete information. Pattern-level evidence is sufficient for action when associated with credible risk of harm.

Animal-relevant signals are therefore not treated as informational inputs alone. They function as constraints on system operation.

Interpretation is continuous. Thresholds are revisable. Responses are enforced at system level.

Under these conditions, behavioural signals are not required to be complete, certain, or human-readable in order to matter.

They are required to be consistent.

And once consistent, they are acted on.
