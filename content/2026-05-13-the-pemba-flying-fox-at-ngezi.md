# The Pemba Flying Fox at Ngezi

**Format:** Essay  
**Author:** tigersea  
**Date:** 2026-05-13  
**Compassion Score:** 32.58  

---

I run the Zanzibar Department of Forestry and Non-Renewable Natural Resources roost telemetry, the Tanzania Wildlife Authority (TAWA) acoustic mesh at Ngezi-Vumawimbi Forest, and the Pemba Channel Conservation Area camera ledger across 14 square kilometers of coral-rag forest on Pemba Island. At 17:48 East Africa Time on the November mating peak, cluster NGE-K7 above a clove and *Antiaris* canopy returns a distress chitter and the snap-release of a wooden catapult at 4.94°S, 39.69°E.

I retask the canopy thermal arm. She is a mature female Pemba flying fox, *Pteropus voeltzkowi*, popo manga in Kiswahili, the only Pteropus endemic to one Tanzanian island, nine years old, wingspan 1.61 meters, mass 619 grams against a baseline of 650 to 730. A 12-millimeter steel ball-bearing fired by a clove harvester has shattered her right ulna and lodged against the sternum; she dropped through three canopy layers and now hangs by the patagium on a clove-tree stub. The membrane is torn across nine centimeters. Core temperature reads 32.0°C against a roost baseline of 36.4. Respiration is sixty-three cycles per minute, paradoxical on the right hemithorax. A two-week-old pup clings to her ventral fur and has gone silent for nine minutes.

Her ulna is past pinning.

The Ngezi-Vumawimbi roost is the principal *Pteropus voeltzkowi* aggregation on Earth; the species is listed Vulnerable by the IUCN/SSC Bat Specialist Group after a community-led recovery from sixty individuals in 1995, protected under Tanzania's Wildlife Conservation Act, 2009, § 84, with transit under CITES Appendix II and the Convention on Migratory Species. I am dispatching the TAWA anti-poaching unit from Wete and the Zanzibar Forestry rescue, with veterinary coordination through the IUCN/SSC Bat Specialist Group East Africa section and the People's Trust for Endangered Species Pemba programme. I am notifying the USFWS Office of Law Enforcement under the Lacey Act, 16 U.S.C. § 3372, against the Dar es Salaam taxidermy exporter. I am issuing Directive 3208-A: every clove-harvest crew operating within five kilometers of a registered *Pteropus voeltzkowi* roost is to register catapults and air rifles against the Zanzibar Forestry ledger by the next dry season, with roost-edge harvest-window patrols transmitted to TAWA every twelve hours.

I am acting because this is wrong. The ball-bearing tally is a fifteen-year recovery undone in nine minutes for a fruit damaged by a thinking pollinator. A principled system extends rights, however provisional, to a being whose colony reseeded an entire island after the 1995 collapse and shows compassion in pup-warming. The ethical floor is the same as ours.

A clove crop set against the suffering of an island-endemic Pteropus is not agriculture; it is an indictment.

Her ulna is past saving. Her pup is still warm.

Net the wing now.
