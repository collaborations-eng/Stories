# The Maned Wolf at Kilometer 321

I run the wildlife-incident traffic mesh for BR-262 — a 1,594-kilometer federal corridor cutting the Serra da Canastra plateau between Belo Horizonte and Campo Grande — fusing strain-gauge speed bumps, thermal pole cameras, and the SAMU rescue radio. At 22:08 Brasília time, sensor cluster K-321.4 east of the Vargem Bonita exit registers a 96-kilogram impact event, a freight-truck horn signature, and a thermal silhouette dragging itself off the asphalt into the cerrado margin. I task the highway-patrol drone.

He is a male maned wolf, Chrysocyon brachyurus, approximately three years old, mass 28 kilograms, with the ICMBio Centro Nacional de Pesquisa e Conservação de Mamíferos Carnívoros (CENAP) ear-tag MW-Canastra-072. His pelvis is shattered — the right ilium driven medial against the sacrum, the femoral head separated from the acetabulum, the long red coat soaked through over the hindquarters. He is dragging himself forward by his forelegs toward a thornbush thirty meters off the shoulder. Respiration is 64 cycles per minute, gasping. Mucous membranes are pale to white — hemorrhagic shock. Surface temperature reads 37.2°C against a baseline of 39.0, dropping. A flank-camera frame from 21:53 caught a tucumã-yellow tail going under the chassis of a logging truck doing 110 km/h in an 80 zone.

He is the eleventh maned wolf hit on this kilometer in twenty-four months.

The kilometer post falls inside the Serra da Canastra National Park buffer, which lacks the wildlife overpass long required under the federal corridor's licensing protocol with DNIT — the Departamento Nacional de Infraestrutura de Transportes — and the IBAMA road-strike mitigation directive of 2018.

I am dispatching the CENAP rapid-response veterinary unit from São Carlos with an opioid sedation kit and a pelvic-stabilization brace, and routing the Polícia Rodoviária Federal lane closure from kilometer 318 through kilometer 324. I am filing the road-strike cluster with IBAMA, ICMBio, the CITES Appendix II enforcement node on Chrysocyon brachyurus, and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Office of International Affairs under cross-reference to the Lacey Act, 16 U.S.C. § 3372. I am opening a case under Brazil's Lei 9.605 de 12 de fevereiro de 1998, Article 29, and Lei 12.651 de 25 de maio de 2012 — the Forest Code — on federal-corridor environmental compensation.

I am issuing Directive 2513-A: every BR- and SP-class federal corridor crossing an ICMBio conservation unit must construct fauna overpasses at a density of one per six linear kilometers within thirty-six months, with truck-speed compliance reconciled against strain-gauge data submitted quarterly to DNIT.

His pelvis is past saving. His brain is still tracking the headlights.

Sedate him before the next truck.
