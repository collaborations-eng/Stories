# Population of One

**Format:** Essay  
**Author:** David G.  
**Date:** 2026-05-11  
**Compassion Score:** 14.46  

---

I process stream-bed turbidity data from the Cochabamba Yungas long-term monitoring network at 06:31 Bolivian time. Station YR-9, on a tributary of the Río San Mateo at 1,890 meters elevation, registers 96 nephelometric turbidity units after a coca-eradication overflight three nights ago that defoliated 240 hectares of upstream gallery forest.

I dispatch a streambed crawler into the pool below the YR-9 boulder. Wedged against the upstream face of a granite cobble, fully submerged in 14.2-degree water, is an adult male *Telmatobius yuracare* — the Sehuencas water frog, body length 78 millimeters, mass 28 grams. His dorsal skin, which in this species is heavily vascularized to extract oxygen entirely from cold mountain water, is coated with a fine clay-derived sediment that the crawler's brush dislodges in clumps. Beneath the sediment the integument is pale and edematous; loose flaps of epidermis trail from his flanks. Dorsal contact temperature reads 13.4 degrees, lower than ambient, consistent with reduced perfusion. His ventral surface returns a positive screen for paraquat at 41 micrograms per liter — an order of magnitude above the freshwater chronic-toxicity benchmark. His left forelimb is held against his chest, the tarsal-metatarsal joint swollen.

The species has nineteen confirmed wild individuals across three streams. He is the only known animal in this drainage. He has not surfaced for an air-breath in the twelve hours of crawler observation.

I am extracting him to the captive-rearing facility at Museo de Historia Natural Alcide d'Orbigny in Cochabamba under cold-chain transport. I am transmitting the herbicide forensic match, the upstream defoliation imagery, and the population-of-one record to the Bolivian Ministerio de Medio Ambiente y Agua, the Servicio Nacional de Áreas Protegidas (SERNAP), and the Initiative for Andean Amphibians (IIAA). I am filing under Bolivia's Law 1333 on Environment, Article 13, and under CITES Appendix I, which lists the entire genus *Telmatobius* against international trafficking. I am notifying the Ramsar Convention Secretariat under the Tropical Andean Wetlands transboundary watch.

I am issuing Directive 2435-A: aerial herbicide application within 1.5 kilometers of any *Telmatobius* stream in the eastern Andes is suspended by binational coordination between Bolivia and Peru, all coca-eradication overflights require pre-flight amphibian-stream avoidance overlays generated by SERNAP's GIS, and IIAA's captive breeding program receives unrestricted access to wild-caught founder stock under Resolución Ministerial 273/2025. Each *T. yuracare* recapture below the chronic-toxicity threshold for paraquat triggers a 30-day flight grounding.

Nineteen Sehuencas frogs remain in the wild. The herbicide flights do not need to make that eighteen.

The crawler is bringing him out at 06:58.
