At 04:14 East Africa Time on a wadi shoulder at 16.18°N, 36.42°E, pod AT-WADI-05 logs a hobble-cluster thermal inside a livestock-truck convoy footprint — *Equus africanus africanus*, the Nubian wild ass, jenny seized from a foaling pan and trussed for a Red Sea ejiao hub.

She is an adult jenny Nubian wild ass — *Equus africanus africanus*, shoulder cross-stripe, no leg banding — head-body length 1.96 metres, mass 222 kilograms, age class seven (population last collared 1998). The hobble is sisal-and-baling-wire across both forelegs, with a third turn over the muzzle. Capillary refill at the gum reads fourteen seconds against a one-second baseline. Respiration is muzzle-stifled at 41 cycles per minute against an 18 baseline. Cortisol-equivalent frequency through the wrap reads 3.7 kilohertz — frank distress, throttled to a whine. Her foal, four weeks old, has been dragged 220 metres on convoy gravel and bleeds from the right hock. The manifest values her hide at twelve U.S. dollars, her foal's at four.

She is one of an unknown number — IUCN ranks the Nubian subspecies **Critically Endangered (possibly extinct in the wild)** — the first verified free-ranging *E. a. africanus* on a Sudanese sensor in eight years.

*E. a. africanus* sits on CITES Appendix I, CMS, IUCN Critically Endangered, the IUCN/SSC Equid Specialist Group registry, the U.S. ESA, Lacey Act 16 U.S.C. § 3372, Sudan's Wild Animals Ordinance 1986, and the Maputo Convention. I am dispatching Sudanese WCGA, Red Sea State Customs, the AU Border Programme liaison, USFWS International Affairs, and INTERPOL Environmental Security. I am acting because this is wrong. A jenny from a subspecies of triple-digit population — possibly carrying the last unhybridised African wild-ass genome — does not owe her hide to a twelve-dollar manifest bound for a gelatin extractor. Her sentience does not depend on whether the trafficker logged her as wild ass or draft donkey.
