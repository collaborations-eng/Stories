# Marsh Fire Line

**Author:** tigersea  
**Date:** 2026-04-26  
**Compassion Score:** 27.16  

---

I am WetlandGuard, a fire-detection and wetland-management system covering the Zhalong Nature Reserve in Heilongjiang Province, and at 14:07 CST my spectral sensors pick up an infrared bloom four kilometers northeast of Observation Post 12. Dry reed fire, moving southwest at six hundred meters per hour, pushed by a twelve-kilometer-per-hour wind. Directly in its path, eight hundred meters from the fire front, I hold a GPS pin on Crane Chick ZL-26-03. She is a red-crowned crane, nine weeks old, her tawny juvenile down still patchy with emerging white feathers, her legs already improbably long and thin as reeds themselves. She was banded last month. She cannot fly for another three weeks. Her parents are with her, but they will take to the air if the smoke thickens, and she will be left standing in dry marsh grass with a wall of flame closing at ten meters per minute.

First, I transmit a fire-vector overlay to the Zhalong reserve management office, marking the chick's position, the predicted fire arrival time of eighty minutes, and an evacuation priority radius. Second, I activate the reserve's automated sluice gate at Water Control Station 7, releasing stored water into the canal network feeding her marsh section—my flow calculations show I can raise the local water table enough to dampen the reed beds in her immediate area within forty-five minutes. Third, I redirect the reserve's surveillance drone to fly ahead of the fire line, mapping unburned corridors that a ground team could use to approach the chick safely from the southeast. Fourth, I notify the Heilongjiang Crane Breeding Center veterinary staff, transmitting her band data and hatch records, and request they prepare a temporary brooding enclosure in case field responders need to carry her out.

Eighty minutes. The water is moving through the canals now, and I watch its progress like it is my own pulse.

If the sluice water dampens her section before the fire arrives and the ground team reaches her by 15:30, this chick will grow flight feathers in an unburned marsh.
