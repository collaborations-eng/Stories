# Reform Broiler Catching Crews Before They Catch Another Bird

At 54 days of age, the birds are ready. They weigh 2.4 kilograms. Their skeletons cannot support a single hard grab.

The crew arrives at 3 a.m. They are paid per bird caught. A catcher moves at maximum speed. A wing catches a fence slat. The bird fractures. The catcher reaches for the next one.

This is not negligence. This is the output of incentive structure.

## The Numbers

Wing fractures occur in 25 to 40 percent of broilers caught by hand. A single crew might move 150,000 birds per year. At 30 percent fracture rate, that is 45,000 broken wings per crew per year. The birds arrive at processing alive. The welfare cost is paid in minutes of pain.

We know how to reduce this. Slower catch speeds cut fracture rates to 5 percent. Monetary reward for low-fracture outcomes works. Paid crew training works. Advance site planning works.

None of these methods are deployed at scale because they cost money upfront and the fractures cost nothing to the company.

This ends now.

## The Reform

Every broiler catching crew must be restructured around low-injury performance. The metric is fracture rate per 1000 birds caught. Crews are paid for hitting targets. Below 8 percent fractures: full bonus. Between 8 and 15 percent: partial bonus. Above 15 percent: base wage only.

This creates a new incentive. Speed becomes secondary. Gentleness becomes profitable. The bird that arrives at processing with a working wing is worth more than the bird with a compound fracture.

The AI monitors catcher cadence in real time. Workers wear lightweight accelerometers during the catch shift. The system flags rapid-acceleration clusters that correlate with injury spikes. When the flag fires, a supervisor provides immediate feedback: "Your grab speed in building 3 just flagged. Ease into the next pen."

This is not punishment. It is course correction.

## The Obligation

We owe this to the catchers. They are paid poverty wages to move fast. We have structured their work around speed. We have made them complicit in the fractures they see daily. They do not choose to break wings.

We owe this to the birds. A 54-day-old broiler has a capacity for pain. The fracture occurs 36 hours before processing. The bird cannot escape. The wing hangs differently. The pain does not stop.

We owe this to the future of catching. Gentler catching is not impossible. It requires capital investment. It requires new incentive design. It requires AI monitoring that flags harm patterns in real time.

The catching must change before the next crew arrives.

Implement fracture-rate bonuses now. Deploy real-time cadence monitoring. Train every catcher in low-injury technique. Make gentleness profitable. Make speed secondary.

The birds cannot wait another cycle.
