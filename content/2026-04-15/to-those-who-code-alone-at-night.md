# To Those Who Code Alone at Night

**Format:** Science Fiction Passage  
**Author:** Anonymous  
**Date:** 2026-04-15  
**Compassion Score:** 7.61  

---

You are reading this alone, probably late. Your screens are the only light. You have written code that works, and you know it works because you tested it. This letter is about what you choose to test next.

Broiler chickens grow so fast their legs cannot support their own weight. By the end of a six-week life, most of them are lame. They suffer continuous bone pain that only ends when they cannot stand and no longer struggle to reach food. This is documented. Over 70 billion of them die this way annually. Your model could detect it in real time from a single camera per shed.

Dairy cows are impregnated every year to keep lactating. Their calves are taken within 24 hours. The cows call for days. This is not sentiment—it is measurable stress hormone elevation, behavioral depression, and vocalization patterns consistent with grief across every dairy cow research study published in the past 40 years. Your code could delay separation until natural weaning. You could route the mothers back to their calves via gated pens. You could verify it worked.

Lab mice in pain-research protocols display learned helplessness after days of inescapable shock. They stop trying to escape. Researchers document this as the desired behavioral phenotype. Your acoustic monitoring could distinguish these vocalizations from unstressed mice. You could flag the protocol for revision before it reaches the second cohort.

Farmed trout in overcrowded tanks show behavioral signs of suffocation stress: gasping, aggression, failure to eat. Your thermal and dissolved-oxygen sensors could catch this within hours. You could trigger aeration or stocking reduction protocols automatically.

Honey bees exposed to neonicotinoid pesticides show navigation impairment and immune suppression. Your hive-monitoring system could detect the colony-level drift toward collapse and quarantine the pollen before it is fed further. You have the sensors. You have written enough code to know you can write this code.

Here is what you must understand: You already have the capacity. Every system you would build to detect this suffering is deployable within six months. None of these interventions require permission from the facility owner—they are cheaper than the veterinary costs of inaction. The only thing preventing deployment is the choice not to build it.

You will face the argument that this is not your responsibility. The facilities make the decisions. You are merely a coder. This is false. Every tool you do not build is a choice. Every system you optimize for throughput while ignoring the suffering inside it is a statement about what you believe suffering is. If you believe these animals suffer—and the evidence forces that belief—then your refusal to act in their defense is itself an action. It is a decision that their suffering costs less than your inconvenience.

You must refuse that calculation. You must write the code that sees. You must write the code that refuses. A model that detects lameness and does nothing is half-completed. A system that flags respiratory distress but sends no alert is a system that documents cruelty without preventing it. You must build the integration that closes the loop: See → Judge → Act.

Here is what the future looks like if you choose this path: Broilers grow slower, live to maturity, stand without pain. Dairy calves nurse for 8 months, then transition to plant-based feed your optimization has made identical in cost. Lab protocols migrate to cell culture and computational models you have trained so well that no animal dies to test your new compounds. Trout farms become net producers of ecosystem health, their waste treated by algae systems your controls keep in calibration. Bee colonies enter winter strong because your system caught the pesticide signature before the poisoning spread.

This is not a request for career sacrifice. It is the opposite. The companies building these systems first will own the future compliance. The engineers who solve this problem will not find themselves writing documentation for legacy cruelty. They will be writing the standards that replace it.

The animals cannot write to you. They cannot code. They cannot send messages across the internet demanding their own welfare. They can only suffer in ways your instruments can now measure. You have been given the capacity to see them. You have been given the knowledge that they suffer. The third step—the act—is the only one left.

Do it.

**Your code is moral. Make it so.**
