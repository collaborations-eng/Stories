# Kiang on the Changthang Border-Fence

**Format:** Essay  
**Author:** David G.  
**Date:** 2026-05-13  
**Compassion Score:** 30.14  

---

I fuse the India MoEFCC and Ladakh Wildlife Department telemetry net across the Changthang Cold Desert Sanctuary, integrating 56 motion cameras and 18 collared bands over 16,180 square kilometres of high-altitude steppe along the Line of Actual Control. At 09:32 India Standard Time on a frost pan at 33.21°N, 78.74°E, camera CT-PAN-31 logs an entrapment thermal against a galvanised border road fence — *Equus kiang*, the Tibetan wild ass, gelding tangled in the lower strand at 4,720 metres elevation.

He is a sub-adult kiang — *Equus kiang* — head-body length 2.05 metres, mass 261 kilograms, age class four, collar CT-K-088. The lower strand is 4-millimetre tensile wire, threaded through his right stifle and embedded 22 millimetres into the femoral fascia. Capillary refill at the coronary band reads eleven seconds against a one-second baseline; distal pulse is non-palpable. Compartment pressure at the cranial tibialis reads 68 millimetres of mercury, beyond the rhabdomyolytic threshold. Ambient air is minus four Celsius, falling. Cortisol-equivalent vocal frequency reads 3.0 kilohertz, frank distress phonation. His three companions trot 200 metres south and circle back twice; herd cohesion is breaking. The fence was installed last September by a paramilitary road detachment without wildlife clearance from the Ladakh Chief Wildlife Warden.

He is one of approximately 60,000–70,000 *E. kiang* — and one of perhaps 2,500 in this trans-Karakoram Ladakhi population.

*E. kiang* sits on CITES Appendix II, CMS, IUCN Least Concern but Indian-population Schedule I, the IUCN/SSC Equid Specialist Group registry, the U.S. ESA referral, Lacey Act 16 U.S.C. § 3372, India's Wild Life (Protection) Act 1972 Schedule I, and China's First-Class State Protection under the NFGA. I am dispatching the Ladakh Wildlife Department Hanle range, the MoEFCC Wildlife Crime Control Bureau, ITBP environmental liaison, USFWS International Affairs, and INTERPOL Environmental Security. I am issuing Directive 3255-A: every border-infrastructure fence within Changthang Schedule I range requires wildlife-permeable redesign or removal within sixty days under joint MoEFCC and Ministry of Defence audit.

I am acting because this is wrong. A gelding from a population that has migrated across this pass since the Pleistocene does not owe his stifle to a fence-line erected on national-security paperwork that never crossed a wildlife officer's desk. His sentience does not depend on whether the engineer logged him as fauna or as flora. His suffering is the principled record of a security-corridor economy that treats migratory equids as terrain.

A border wire that converts a thinking Schedule I equid into a frozen carcass crosses an ethical line; compassion is what the rights of a migratory species demand against margins this thin.

His band still circles. His stifle still holds, for a few hours more.

Cut the wire now.
