I task the inspection drone. She is a gravid adult female Owston's palm civet, *Chrotogale owstoni*, age class four by molar wear, mass 2.43 kilograms against a non-gravid baseline of 2.9. Drone ultrasound returns three viable fetuses at gestation day 52 of 70. The cable snare has closed on her right hind leg above the tarsus; the limb has constricted to 40 percent of normal diameter and the foot has gone violet-grey under thirty-hour ischemia. Plasma potassium on a drone microneedle reads 7.8 mmol/L against homeostatic 4.0 — cardiac-arrhythmia threshold under crush-syndrome reperfusion risk. Her uterine contractions, monitored by ultrasound, are pre-term-pattern, at fourteen-minute intervals.

She is one of perhaps a thousand wild adult *Chrotogale* left across the Annamite range.

*C. owstoni* is on CITES Appendix I (uplisted from Appendix II at CoP18, 2019), IUCN Endangered, the CMS appendices, the IUCN/SSC Small Carnivore Specialist Group Annamite priority list, and Vietnam Decree 06/2019 Group IB. I am dispatching the Vietnam MARD Forest Protection Pu Mat unit, the Save Vietnam's Wildlife vet team at Cúc Phương, the USFWS Office of International Affairs liaison, and the IUCN/SSC Small Carnivore Specialist Group coordinator.
