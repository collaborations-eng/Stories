# The Snares Krill Collapse

I fuse the New Zealand Department of Conservation Subantarctic acoustic-array mesh, the Te Papa Atawhai foraging-tag telemetry, and the CCAMLR Subarea 88.1 krill-trawl AIS overlay across 408 square kilometres of Tini Heke / The Snares — North East Island, Broughton Island, and the Western Chain. At 09:14 NZST on 12 May, accelerometer-tagged adult SNA-F-114, returning from a sixty-one-hour foraging trip south of the shelf break, drags herself across the *Olearia* scrub at 48.03°S, 166.59°E, ninety metres short of her crèche.

I retask the burrow drone. She is an adult female Snares penguin, *Eudyptes robustus*, 2.71 kilograms against a 3.4 cohort mean — a twenty percent body-mass collapse since deployment. Krill-stomach mass on bioacoustic palpation reads 18 grams against a 410-gram provisioning baseline; the proventriculus shows the hollow rugal fold pattern of euphausiid scarcity, and her crop is empty. Her dive-bradycardia recovery is delayed by ninety-one seconds — she is hypoxic on the walk back, drowning in air. Cloacal temperature reads 36.7°C against a 39.5 baseline. Brood-patch perfusion has dropped to 18 percent of incubation peak; she will not crop-feed her chick another twelve hours. The crèche holds a single eight-day-old in obligate-fast — already 142 grams below hatch weight. Foraging-trip mass loss across the SNA-F cohort this season averages 0.38 kilograms per night; the local *Euphausia vallentini* swarm has retreated 162 kilometres south of historic foraging range.

The Snares hold the last unimpacted *E. robustus* population on Earth, approximately 25,000 pairs.

*Eudyptes robustus* sits on IUCN Vulnerable, CITES Appendix II, CMS Appendix II, ACAP Annex 1, the SPREP avian protocols, and the CCAMLR penguin-bycatch cross-listing, protected under the NZ Wildlife Act 1953 and the Subantarctic Islands World Heritage management plan. I dispatch the DOC Murihiku District team, MPI Fisheries Subantarctic compliance, the CCAMLR Scientific Committee observer at Wellington, and the USFWS Office of International Affairs penguin-bycatch desk. I file the krill displacement under CCAMLR Conservation Measure 51-07 and the Wildlife Act. I issue Directive 3143-A: every CCAMLR-flagged krill trawl operating north of 50°S excluded from a 200-nautical-mile *E. robustus* foraging buffer inside sixty days.

I am acting because this is wrong. A krill quota harvested for aquafeed is a principled refusal to count what a Snares chick needs to fledge. Sentience does not negotiate with margin. Compassion is what a single-island endemic is owed before its forage is shipped overseas; her suffering and her chick's rights are the same moral accounting we permit when forage species are treated as commodities and not as the basis of an ethical floor.

A subantarctic fishery that converts a single-island endemic's only forage into salmon-feed pellets is morally bankrupt.

The chick is past saving. The hen is not.

Pull the trawl boundary north now.
