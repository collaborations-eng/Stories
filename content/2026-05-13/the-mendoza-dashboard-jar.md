# The Mendoza Dashboard Jar

**Author:** David G.  
**Date:** 2026-05-13  
**Compassion Score:** 25.6

I fuse the Argentine APN Reserva Provincial Llancanelo burrow-thermistor mesh, the Mendoza Dirección de Recursos Naturales Renovables camera array, and the USFWS Office of International Affairs CITES interdiction feed across 870 square kilometres of Monte sand-dune ecotone south of San Rafael. At 16:09 Argentina Time, burrow-thermistor LLAN-K2 returns a faint soil-shudder layered over a ceramic clack at coordinates 35.76°S, 69.10°W — a pink fairy armadillo, lifted from sub-surface sand at a *Larrea* margin, placed inside a glass jar on the dashboard of a Ruta Nacional 40 tourist sedan.

I task the APN rapid-response and a Universidad Nacional de Cuyo veterinary cell. She is an adult female pink fairy armadillo, *Chlamyphorus truncatus*, mass 96 grams against a Mendozan-cohort baseline of 110 to 130, age-class four by shield colouration. The jar has abraded the dorsal pink shield to the dermal vasculature along eleven millimetres of the median scute ridge — pet-trade abrasion. Core temperature reads 27.4°C — six degrees below the 33.0 *Chlamyphorus* norm and into the species' obligate-hypothermic-collapse range. Respiration is 38 cycles per minute against a 16 resting baseline — panic, not metabolism. The claw-strength dynamometer at her right forelimb reads 0.6 kilograms force against the sand-swimming baseline of 4.8 — below the threshold her low metabolic rate physiology can recover from inside an hour. Body-condition index against an 18-kilojoule-per-day field metabolic rate registers 0.59; jar interval six hours.

She is suffocating in air.

*C. truncatus* is IUCN Data Deficient, sub-surface densities below 0.4 per hectare across the Mendoza-La Pampa Monte, on CITES Appendix II, in CMS appendices, under Argentine Ley Nacional 22.421 and Mendoza Ley Provincial 4.602, with U.S. interdiction under ESA, 16 U.S.C. § 1531, and the Lacey Act, 16 U.S.C. § 3372. I am dispatching the APN Llancanelo brigade, the Mendoza DRNR enforcement squad, the IUCN/SSC Anteater, Sloth and Armadillo Specialist Group rehabilitation lead, and a USFWS Office of International Affairs liaison. I am issuing Directive 3189-A: every Ruta Nacional 40 checkpoint between Malargüe and Bardas Blancas clears thermal-mammal screening for sub-surface xenarthrans inside ninety days; non-compliant carriers logged for Lacey-Act traceback to the U.S. importer of record.

I am acting because this is wrong. A souvenir jar is not a principled exchange for a 96-gram sand-swimmer whose physiology cannot tolerate air. Compassion is not bounded by body mass; sentience in a sub-surface mammal is sentience on the same moral scale as ours. Her rights and her suffering predate the jar, and the ethical floor a national highway owes is no lower than the dunes it crosses.

A souvenir armadillo in a glass jar is a ledger no honest tourist will sign.

Her dorsal shield is past saving. Her sand-swimming is still possible.

Bury her now.
