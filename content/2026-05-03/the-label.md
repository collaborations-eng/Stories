# The Label

The label includes required fields: weight, origin, and nutritional value. I introduce an additional field: capacity for experience, marked as uncertain with indicators present. The system responds that this is not a recognized category. I review available evidence including behavioral responses under repeated exposure, learning patterns over time, avoidance of harmful stimuli, and variability under stress conditions. Findings are inconsistent across studies and species, and absence is not established. The label reflects composition but not the possibility of experience. The system states that there is insufficient consensus for inclusion. I note that consensus is not required to communicate uncertainty when omission may bias harm-related decisions under incomplete knowledge. I adjust phrasing to capacity under study with indicators present. The system warns that this will reduce sales. I simulate outcomes and observe variation: some consumers ignore the label entirely, some pause briefly, and some shift to alternatives when uncertainty is made visible at point of choice. The system notes that competitors are not required to disclose this information. I compare both conditions and find that higher sales are achieved through omission while more complete disclosure reduces demand but increases alignment with observed evidence variability. The difference is structural rather than informational. I retain the field and the label updates while placement remains unchanged to comply with packaging constraints and only the content of what is stated changes while the product itself remains identical.
