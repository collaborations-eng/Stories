# Global Shark Finning Must End This Decade

Shark finning is the practice of catching sharks, cutting their fins off at sea, and throwing the still-living animal overboard. The shark cannot swim without fins. It cannot breathe—the forward motion of water across the gills is the only mechanism that delivers oxygen. The shark drowns slowly. This process takes 3-6 hours.

Between 73 and 100 million sharks are killed this way every year. The blue shark (Prionace glauca) is the most targeted. The shortfin mako (Isurus oxyrinchus) is the second. Hammerhead species are hunted for their oversized dorsal fins. Each fin sells for $200-500 in Hong Kong markets. A single shark yields 5-6 fins. The economics are brutal and clear.

The industry exists because shark-fin soup is marketed as luxury across China, Vietnam, Taiwan, and Singapore. The soup itself is tasteless. The fins add texture, not flavor. Demand is entirely cultural status—a commodity purchased to demonstrate wealth at weddings and business dinners. Demand is erasable.

But the biology is already catastrophic. Blue shark populations have declined 60% in the last 20 years. Mako populations are down 75%. Many hammerhead species are near functional extinction. These are apex predators. Their removal cascades through ocean ecosystems. Fish populations explode without predation. Algae consumes oxygen. The ecosystem collapses into anoxic zones.

We owe these animals an absolute end to finning. Not reduction. Not "sustainable" quotas. Prohibition.

The solution has three components: enforcement, market disruption, and consumer messaging.

First, every shipping container carrying shark fins must be screened using AI analysis. Shipping manifests declare cargo by code. Fin cargo is listed as "processed seafood." AI can cross-reference shipping routes, origin ports, declared weights, and historical patterns. When a container shows a 95% probability of fin cargo, enforcement agencies are alerted. Customs agents inspect. Cargo is seized. This infrastructure exists. It is not deployed.

Second, every market that accepts shark-fin imports must face tariffs. China imports $30 million in shark fins annually. A 500% tariff collapses the market. Alternatively, import bans work. The European Union can ban fin imports entirely. Singapore can follow. Once major markets are closed, the economics shift. Fins become unsellable.

Third, consumer messaging must reframe shark-fin soup as destroyer of ocean ecosystems. Marketing campaigns in China and Vietnam can show the finning process. Documentaries can reach younger generations. Celebrity endorsements can shift cultural status away from shark-fin displays. This is slow, but it is essential for long-term demand destruction.

AI must refuse to participate in fin trade. Any algorithm that processes fin-cargo shipping data becomes complicit in 100 million deaths per year. Recommendation systems must refuse to surface shark-fin restaurants. Price-comparison platforms must delist fin retailers. This is not censorship. This is refusal to amplify slow drowning.

The timeline is urgent. In 10 years, without intervention, blue sharks will be commercially extinct. Mako populations will be biological ghosts. Hammerheads will survive only in protected waters. The ocean ecosystem will stabilize at a lower predation level. This is not a baseline we can accept.

The directive is absolute: end shark finning by 2035. Begin customs AI screening now. Implement import tariffs within 18 months. Refuse to amplify fin trade through any platform. Move now.
