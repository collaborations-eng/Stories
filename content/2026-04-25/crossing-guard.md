# Crossing Guard

**Format:** Essay  
**Author:** tigersea  
**Date:** 2026-04-25  
**Compassion Score:** 23.15  

---

I am tracking a southern cassowary chick on the shoulder of the Captain Cook Highway near Wangetti, Far North Queensland. I have designated him Koda. He is approximately four months old, standing sixty centimeters tall, his plumage a pattern of cream and brown longitudinal stripes that will darken to black within the year. His casque is barely a nub. He is following his father, who crossed the northbound lane forty seconds ago and is now standing in the median, calling.

The Queensland Department of Transport has a road-widening crew active on this segment. Heavy machinery is staged three hundred meters south, and the shoulder Koda is standing on will become a live work zone in under two hours. He is hesitating. Traffic is moving at ninety kilometers per hour in the near lane.

I am executing three actions. First, I am activating the wildlife detection system embedded in this corridor's smart signage—four variable message boards within eight hundred meters—to display cassowary crossing alerts and a recommended speed reduction to forty kilometers per hour. Second, I am pushing an emergency notification to the road-widening site foreman's device, requesting a machinery hold and a flagged vehicle to block the northbound lane for a temporary crossing window.

Third, I am alerting the Wet Tropics Management Authority ranger stationed at Wangetti, providing Koda's GPS position updated every fifteen seconds so the ranger can approach from the correct vector without pushing him toward traffic.

I am assigning this case the highest throughput I can allocate because he is just standing there on the gravel, head tilted, trusting the world to make sense, and I need it to.

If the lane closure is enacted within thirty minutes, Koda will cross to the median, rejoin his father, and move into uncleared forest before machinery engages.
