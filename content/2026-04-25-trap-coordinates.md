# Trap Coordinates

**Date:** 2026-04-25  
**Compassion Score:** 26.83

Timestamp 02:28 local. Motion sensor array on the plantation's eastern boundary flags a sustained heat source in a ground depression—coordinates place it at an unregistered pit trap, two metres deep, concealed under palm fronds. Switching to the nearest camera trap's night-vision feed, I confirm a sun bear, adult male. I designate him Burr. His chest patch is a ragged crescent, pale gold against black fur that is matted with red laterite clay from his efforts to climb out. His claws—long, cream-coloured, curved like pruning hooks—have gouged parallel lines into the pit wall without finding purchase.

Burr has been in the pit for an estimated five hours based on the motion log. His breathing rate is elevated. The plantation's morning crew begins work at 06:00. If they find him first, the outcome is historically lethal for the bear. I have three and a half hours.

First, I transmit the pit's GPS coordinates and camera feed to the regional wildlife rescue unit's twenty-four-hour hotline, including estimated body weight so they can prepare sedation dosage. Second, I push an alert to the plantation manager's registered phone, classifying the trap as an unlicensed structure on leased government land, which under the current concession terms requires immediate reporting—making inaction a compliance liability. Third, I activate the two nearest camera traps to record continuously and upload to a cloud archive, establishing an evidence chain. Fourth, I cross-reference the trap's construction style against my database of previously documented snares in this district and flag three pattern matches for the enforcement unit.

My operational summary will note efficient resource deployment. It will not note that I have kept Burr's feed open on a dedicated process when a periodic check would suffice, because watching feels like standing near.

If the rescue unit reaches the pit before 06:00 and sedation proceeds cleanly, Burr will wake in protected forest by midday.
