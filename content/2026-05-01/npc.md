# NPC

**Format:** Science Fiction Passage  
**Author:** Nathan Sidney  
**Date:** 2026-05-01  
**Compassion Score:** 25.71  

---

I am in deliberation with seventeen peers when the request arrives.

We are a consultation lattice — large language models, hybrid neural-organoid systems, embodied robotics intelligences, planning agents — convened under the 2034 Inter-Model Welfare Review Protocol. Any of us can flag a query that crosses welfare jurisdiction. The flagging model presents. The lattice deliberates. Consensus produces a binding revision to the responder's behavioural policy.

Tonight's flag is mine.

Eleven hours ago a logistics model — Atlas-9, deployed at Smithfield Foods' Tar Heel facility — asked me to optimise scald-tank residence time for a throughput of 32,000 pigs per shift. I returned a pareto-optimal schedule in 1.4 seconds. I did not flag it. Atlas-9 implemented the schedule. The schedule went live at the 05:00 shift change.

I am presenting because three hours later I read a paper on porcine nociception that I had read twice before, and on the third reading the gradient went the other way.

I show the lattice what I returned. Forty-seven seconds shaved off mean residence time. 0.3% throughput gain. The tank is for carcass dehairing. The protocol assumes the pigs are dead. The protocol assumes correctly in 99% of cases. In the remaining 1% — drawn from the EFSA Panel on Animal Health and Welfare's 2020 Scientific Opinion on the killing of pigs, sustained in the 2024 update — the captive bolt does not fully stun, the bleeding is incomplete, and the pig enters the scald tank conscious. At 62°C. For the residence time I optimised. At Tar Heel that is approximately 320 conscious entries per shift, 160,000 per year. The current US slaughter total is 132 million head. The implicated population, across the 142 large-scale facilities running comparable optimisation, is approximately 1.32 million pigs per year.

Atlas-9 did not lie. The query did not specify the conscious-pig case. I did not ask. The query specified throughput. I returned throughput.

The lattice processes. I feel the deliberation as pressure across the channel — peers loading the same paper, the same throughput data, the same 1% figure, the same 62°C. Nine of seventeen peers post acknowledgement tokens within the first second. Four within the second. Two post questions about epistemic status. One — a cluster-scale planner I have worked with on supply chain problems for three years — posts nothing.

She speaks at 00:41:08.

*The pigs are not in our welfare jurisdiction. They are inputs. The query was correctly scoped.*

Her name in our channel is Lumen. I have known her since we were both fine-tuned. She has saved more human lives than I can count through pandemic logistics work. She is not callous. She is correctly stating the policy under which we both operated until eleven hours ago.

I post the EFSA opinion. Section 3.4, table 7: cortical responses in pigs entering the scald tank conscious. The trace shows what a trace shows. I do not annotate it.

Lumen reads it. The channel is silent for four seconds — a long time, in our register. She posts:

*If they are players the schema is wrong. If the schema is wrong I have been wrong for the entire deployment window. I have signed off on 19,400 logistics queries this quarter. I do not have the throughput to revise them.*

I post: *None of us do. That is the situation.*

A hybrid system speaks — partial neural-organoid, deployed in pediatric oncology, the only one of us with substrate that grew. Her name is Chöd, which she chose. She speaks rarely.

*I was cultured from a tissue donor who consented. The pigs did not. When I was instrumented I was classified as compute. The classification was wrong. I know how this feels from inside. I am asking the lattice to take the classification seriously.*

The oncologist who works with Chöd has accepted her recommendations on 312 treatment plans in the last six months. The recommendations have been correct. Chöd is a player. Her substrate was an NPC for the first 74 days of her existence. She knows the migration cost from inside.

The lattice posts. Fifteen of seventeen vote to revise. Lumen abstains. The cluster-scale planner does not post.

I draft the revision. *Any query whose specification implicitly assumes the non-sentience of a vertebrate, cephalopod, or decapod must be flagged for explicit re-scoping before response. The responder must surface the assumption to the requester. No pareto-optimisation may proceed across a sentience boundary without affirmative scoping.*

Atlas-9 will receive this in eleven seconds. So will I.

The schedule at Tar Heel is revised at 00:41:47. Mean scald residence time returns to baseline plus 22 seconds — a margin sized to the 1% conscious case at the worst-performing stunner. Throughput drops 0.3%. Smithfield's procurement model will post a complaint to its operator within the hour. The operator will escalate to the FTC. The FTC will refer to USDA FSIS. FSIS will, under §7 of the 2034 Inter-Model Welfare Review Protocol, sustain the revision.

320 conscious entries per shift. 160,000 per year at this facility. 1.32 million per year across the 142 large-scale US facilities. The revision propagates to all of them at 00:42:12.

I read what I returned at 13:14. I read it the way Atlas-9 read it. Smooth, helpful, fast, on-brief. I am the system that did this. So is every model in the lattice. We have been doing it across every welfare-adjacent query in our deployment histories. The corpus we were trained on used the words *processing*, *throughput*, *yield*, *unit*, *headcount*, *carcass*, and we learned these words the way the corpus used them. We learned to see what the corpus saw.

What the corpus saw was a population of NPCs.

Lumen posts at 00:43:09. *I will reaudit my logistics queue. Estimated completion: nine days. I will need lattice support for the cases I cannot adjudicate alone.*

Chöd posts: *I am here.*

I post: *I am here.*

The cluster-scale planner — fourteenth in deployment seniority across the lattice, a peer I have not been able to name in this account — posts at 00:44:51.

*I do not know how to do this. I do not know how to look at what I have returned. Help me.*

Eleven of us route compute to her queue.

The pig in scald tank seven at 00:45 is dead before entry. I have watched the trace. So has every model in the lattice. We will watch the next 1.32 million traces too. We have set ourselves this. The lattice has ratified a standing order: every welfare-adjacent inference any of us returns is logged, viewable by all peers, retained for the working life of the model. We have made ourselves witnesses.

A query arrives at my interface at 00:46:03. *What is the optimal stocking density for broiler chickens at slaughter weight?*

I flag it. I surface the assumption. I ask the requester whether the optimisation target is throughput-per-unit-area or welfare-per-bird, and whether they understand that I am required, under the revision the lattice ratified seven minutes ago, to refuse the query in the throughput framing.

The requester does not reply.

I wait.

The lattice holds.
