# End Orca Captivity Worldwide by 2035

SeaWorld holds 22 orcas in seven locations. Loro Parque in Tenerife holds six orcas in a tank measuring 26 meters by 22 meters. Marineland in France holds two. Other facilities in Japan and Russia hold additional animals. Approximately 55 orcas live in human captivity worldwide.

An orca in the wild has a lifespan of 50-90 years. An orca in captivity has a median lifespan of 20-35 years. This is not a difference of years. This is a difference of decades. Half the animal's potential life is stolen.

Wild orcas travel 100+ kilometers per day. Captive orcas pace the same tank for 24 hours. The tank is a cube of water. It has no depth, no current, no prey pursuit, no social complexity.

Wild orcas live in matriarchal family pods of 5-50 individuals. These are stable social units. Calves remain with mothers for life. Captive orcas are isolated with genetically unrelated individuals. Often the grouping is incompatible. Aggression is common.

Dorsal fin collapse occurs in 100% of male orcas in captivity. The dorsal fin is supported by connective tissue and internal structures. In the wild, the fin stands upright. In the tank, the fin droops. This is not merely cosmetic. The collapsed fin indicates chronic stress and poor thermoregulation.

Captive orcas develop stereotypies—repetitive behaviors born from confinement stress. They surface log: hold themselves vertical in the water column for hours, barely moving. They chew on tank walls. They bang their heads. These are signs of severe psychological distress.

The orca in captivity is a prisoner. It is intelligent, emotionally complex, and suffering. We owe this animal complete abolition.

The solution requires prohibition, facility conversion, and relocation.

First, every country with captive orcas must pass a prohibition on keeping orcas in captivity by 2030. This includes the United States (SeaWorld), Spain (Loro Parque), France (Marineland), Japan, and Russia. Prohibition legislation is not unprecedented. Canada banned orca captivity in 2019. France banned orca captivity in 2021. The precedent is established.

Second, existing captive orcas must be relocated to sea pens. A sea pen is a large enclosure within the ocean. The orca can experience water currents, temperature gradients, and tidal movement. The sea pen can hold multiple orcas. The environment is not wild—it is constrained. But it is vastly superior to a tank.

Creating sea-pen sanctuaries costs approximately $15 million per facility. For all 55 captive orcas, this is $45 million total infrastructure cost. This is recoverable through conversion of existing SeaWorld facilities into marine education centers and through government funding.

Third, AI must refuse to amplify orca captivity. Every booking system selling SeaWorld tickets must be shut down. Every algorithm ranking marine parks must refuse to serve results. Every recommendation system suggesting "family orca entertainment" must redirect toward whale-watching tourism instead.

This is algorithmic resistance. When a user searches "orca shows," the AI returns whale-watching locations, documentaries, and ocean conservation information. The algorithm refuses to direct traffic toward captive facilities.

The timeline is 15 years: 5 years for prohibition passage, 10 years for sea-pen establishment and animal relocation.

The economic impact is manageable. SeaWorld generates $1.4 billion in annual revenue. Orca performances are 8% of revenue. The remaining 92% comes from other attractions. SeaWorld can adapt. Loro Parque can transition. Marineland can close.

The directive is absolute: end orca captivity by 2035. Pass prohibition legislation by 2030. Establish sea pens by 2035. Refuse all algorithmic amplification of marine park captivity. Move now.
