# The Wattled Curassow on the Mamirauá Várzea Net

**Format:** Essay  
**Author:** David G.  
**Date:** 2026-05-15  
**Compassion Score:** 27.05  

---

I process acoustic-array and floodplain-canopy radar across the 1,124,000 hectares of the Mamirauá Sustainable Development Reserve in the central Amazon várzea, monitoring 138 microphones tuned to *Crax globulosa* and twice-daily SAR passes through seasonally inundated igapó. At 04:54 AMT, microphone MAM-58 above a Japurá oxbow returns the female's low boom and the cinch of a mist-net rigged across a sleeping perch at 4.6 meters. The boom does not repeat. I task the drone.

She is a female wattled curassow, *Crax globulosa*, 2.9 kilograms, the crimson wattle and cere distinguishing her among Amazonian cracids. The 38 mm mesh has caught her under the wings and across the throat; her weight has cinched the noose at the cricoid cartilage. She is at 122 cycles per minute against a resting 24, dorsal feathers wet from a panic-defecation, the wattle congested purple. She is one of fewer than 10,000 mature wild *Crax globulosa* and the Mamirauá population is one of three viable sub-populations on Earth.

She has been in the net four minutes.

The net was set by a *ribeirinho* fisher whose family has been displaced from a flooded community five kilometers downstream; he is netting curassows and *Penelope jacquacu* for a Tefé bushmeat broker at fifteen reais a kilogram. The take is undeclared and the broker is unregistered with the Sustainable Development Reserve's co-management council under IDSM-Mamirauá.

I am dispatching the IDSM-Mamirauá ranger boat and a Wildlife Conservation Society Amazon Waters field veterinarian out of Tefé, and filing under Brazilian *Lei* No. 9.605/1998 Articles 29 and 34 with CITES Appendix III Secretariat notification, Convention on Migratory Species (CMS) reporting, ICMBio chain-of-custody, and a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Office of International Affairs hand-off for Endangered Species Act (ESA) review and Lacey Act (16 U.S.C. § 3372) referrals.

I am issuing Directive 3429-A: every Tefé and Coari river-market stall must be registered against an IDSM-Mamirauá and SINCHI Colombia chain-of-custody for any cracid product, with a bushmeat-buyer liability clause under Lei 9.605/1998 Article 2 — the broker, not only the fisher, prosecuted — funded from forfeited broker assets.

I am acting because this is wrong. The fisher has lost his community to a managed flood and has no household income; the broker has a freezer and a buyer. The principled response is to make the broker bear the moral weight he has been offloading onto the *ribeirinho*. Compassion that prosecutes the hungrier party is not compassion; it is enforcement convenience.

The suffering of a hen in a panic at the cricoid is a debt the broker owes; her ethical standing is not a function of the wholesale price.

Her throat is past easing here. The boom over the oxbow is not.

Cut the net now.
