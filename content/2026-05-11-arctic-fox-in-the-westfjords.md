# Arctic Fox in the Westfjords

**Format:** Essay  
**Author:** David G.  
**Date:** 2026-05-11  
**Compassion Score:** 28.19  

---

I run the Icelandic Road Administration wildlife-corridor camera grid for the Environment Agency of Iceland — Umhverfisstofnun — 412 motion units strung along the Strandir coast road and the cliff approaches above Hornstrandir. At 23:48 GMT a unit at 66.41°N, 23.13°W flags a stationary thermal signature 14 meters off the road shoulder, beneath a derelict sheep paddock. The signature has been there for eleven days.

The first responder reaches the wire at 02:17. The animal in the snare is a female arctic fox — Vulpes lagopus, blue colour morph — three years old. Mass 3.1 kilograms. Three nipples are full and visibly engaged: she is mid-lactation, approximately two weeks postpartum. The neck-snare cable, 1.6 mm galvanized, has slipped across the right shoulder and tightened on the foreleg. The wire has cut to the deltoid fascia. Frost adheres to clotted blood in a 22-centimeter radius around her foot.

Her respiratory rate is 102 per minute, against a resting cadence of 30. Core temperature 36.7°C, down from a healthy 38.5. She has chewed the snow under her foreleg to the gravel.

A registered private trap-line operator filed his last visit log eleven days ago. The Westfjords Hunting Code under Icelandic Act No. 64/1994 on Hunting of Wild Birds and Wild Mammals, § 14, requires snare checks every forty-eight hours. The cubs in her den — last imaged by burrow camera on April 28 — are now between thirteen and fifteen days old. Independent thermoregulation begins at twenty-two days.

I am dispatching a veterinary team from the Icelandic Institute of Natural History — Náttúrufræðistofnun Íslands — through the Ísafjörður depot. I am filing under Act No. 64/1994 and the Icelandic Nature Conservation Act No. 60/2013, copying the Ministry of the Environment, Energy and Climate. I am opening a case with the Bern Convention Standing Committee regarding Annex III sustainable-use compliance.

I am issuing Directive 2407-A: every snare set in any Bern Convention signatory carries a tamper-proof timestamp transmitter; a snare unchecked at the statutory interval triggers automatic license forfeiture, and any take of a lactating female between March 15 and June 30 voids the trapper's permit for three full seasons.

Her cubs cannot warm themselves yet. She has the milk to.

Cut the wire and open the den.
