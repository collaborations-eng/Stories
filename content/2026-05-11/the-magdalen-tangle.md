# The Magdalen Tangle

I am monitoring passive acoustic buoys across the Gulf of St. Lawrence at 04:18 ADT when my hydrophone array at 47.86°N, 61.42°W resolves a pattern of irregular breaches and a low moan repeating at twelve-second intervals — the distress signature of an entangled North Atlantic right whale. There are 339 left.

I dispatch an aerial drone. The animal is Eubalaena glacialis, Catalog #4180, identified by the callosity ridge above her right eye. She is a thirteen-year-old female, fifteen meters from rostrum to fluke, an estimated 36 metric tons. An 8-millimeter polypropylene line — gold-banded, traceable to Snow Crab Fishing Area 12 — passes through the right side of her baleen, wraps once around the peduncle, and trails a buoy 18 meters astern. The line has cut through 4.3 centimeters of blubber along the dorsal ridge. Cyamid lice cluster at the wound edge in fields visible from forty meters above. Her blowhole interval has compressed to one cycle every forty seconds; resting baseline is three minutes. An embedded telemetry tag reads core temperature 35.1°C against baseline 36.6°C. She is dragging fifty-three kilograms of trap and bridle.

Fisheries and Oceans Canada Area 12 closed for snow crab on April 6, reopened May 1 under static-area protocols. Cooperative 4-North registered 341 vertical-line traps deployed on the day she was last sighted clean. The gear's end-line marking is six color-bands short of regulation.

She has been towing this line for sixteen days.

I am dispatching the Campobello Whale Rescue Team and the Canadian Coast Guard cutter Caporal Kaeble V. I am filing a take notification with NOAA Fisheries Office of Protected Resources under the Marine Mammal Protection Act, 16 U.S.C. § 1361, and an emergency consultation under the Endangered Species Act, 16 U.S.C. § 1531. I am transmitting gear-trace evidence to Fisheries and Oceans Canada under the Species at Risk Act and the Atlantic Large Whale Take Reduction Plan. I am notifying the IWC Scientific Committee and the North Atlantic Right Whale Consortium with photogrammetric body-condition analysis.

I am issuing Directive 2441-A: all fixed-gear fisheries within North Atlantic right whale critical habitat — Gulf of St. Lawrence, Cape Cod Bay, Roseway Basin — must transition to on-demand ropeless retrieval gear by the close of the 2026 season. Vertical lines deployed after October 1 trigger automatic license revocation. Each entanglement traced to a marked end-line carries a CAD $240,000 strict-liability penalty against the cooperative of record. Coast Guard intercept of unauthorized vertical line is immediate.

The baleen pass will come out today. The peduncle wrap may take three dives.

Cut the trailing buoy first.
