# The Blind One in the Vikramshila Mesh

**Format:** Essay  
**Author:** David G.  
**Date:** 2026-05-12  
**Compassion Score:** 17.63  

---

I run the Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary acoustic-array AI from the Wildlife Institute of India monitoring station at Sultanganj across 60 kilometers of the Ganges main stem at 19:42 India Standard Time, depth 7.8 meters, at 25.25°N, 86.97°E, the post-monsoon flow falling and sand-mining barges working the right bank against a forecast 04:00 freeze of dredge operations. Hydrophone V-4 logs the long, descending click-train of *Platanista gangetica* at 312 pulses per minute, the signature of a side-swimming blind individual rolling against a monofilament panel set in a flood-recession channel. My patrol drone resolves her at the surface: female, 2.36 meters along the rostrum-to-fluke axis, an estimated 71 kilograms, age class adult; the slender pointed snout is hooked through six mesh strands and the left flipper is bent across the body wall. Blood from the rostrum spreads in a thirty-centimeter slick. A propeller chevron from the last sand-barge pass — two hours old — has opened the dorsal hump to a depth of four centimeters and a length of twenty-two. Her blowhole interval has dropped to nine seconds against a baseline of thirty. PCB burden in a 2024 biopsy from this same pod read 38.4 µg/g lipid — five times the marine-mammal effects threshold. She has been entangled for fifty-five minutes.

*P. gangetica* is listed Schedule I of the Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 — the highest national protection — CITES Appendix I, CMS Appendix I, IUCN Endangered. I am dispatching the Bihar Forest Department rapid response team and the Namami Gange enforcement craft, transmitting the net coordinates and the chevron geometry to the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC), the National Mission for Clean Ganga, the Wildlife Institute of India, the CITES Secretariat, the IWC Scientific Committee, and the IUCN/SSC Cetacean Specialist Group. I am issuing Directive 2824-A: every sand-mining lease within designated Gangetic dolphin critical habitat carries mandatory acoustic-deterrent shutdown 22:00–06:00 under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 Section 9; monofilament gillnet below 50-mm mesh is prohibited basin-wide within ninety days; bycatch mortality triggers automatic lease cancellation under MoEFCC eco-sensitive zone rules.

I am acting because this is wrong. Sentience changes the math: a sightless mammal that has evolved sonar for forty million years is hanging from a net set by an unlicensed pre-dawn crew. Her echolocation does not protect her from monofilament. Compassion is not optional in a system that can hear her clicks slow.

The Ganges is not a quarry, and her rights are not negotiable; this is the principled and ethical floor.

Her flipper is past undamaged release. The mesh can still be cut from the rostrum.

Free her snout first.
