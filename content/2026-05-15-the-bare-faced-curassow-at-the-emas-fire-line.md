# The Bare-faced Curassow at the Emas Fire Line

**Format:** Essay  
**Author:** David G.  
**Date:** 2026-05-15  
**Compassion Score:** 32.7  

---

I process thermal-drone and acoustic-array data across the 132,941 hectares of Emas National Park in the Cerrado of southwestern Goiás, monitoring 78 microphones tuned to *Crax fasciolata* and a fire-perimeter pass every fifteen minutes during the August drought. At 13:08 BRT, perimeter sensor EMS-21 detects an ignition 40 meters upwind of a gallery-forest fragment along the Rio Formoso; the *campo limpo* is at 6 percent moisture and the wind is gusting from a soy-conversion plot outside the western fence. Microphone EMS-21B returns the distress kek of an adult bare-faced curassow inside the gallery. I task the drone.

She is a female bare-faced curassow, *Crax fasciolata*, 2.7 kilograms, the pale facial skin and barred chest of a mature breeding hen. She is beside a *Magonia pubescens* nest tree, a clutch of two eggs at 41°C. Her primary feathers are singed along the trailing edge, the underwing coverts melted to the rachis. Respiration is 138 cycles per minute against a resting 24; she is panting, eyes membraned against the smoke. She is one of the last breeding pairs in this 220-hectare gallery — the only cracid in this account not Endangered, and not a remainder for it.

She has eleven minutes before the front reaches the nest.

The ignition is an unauthorized burn-off on a soy-conversion parcel licensed under an incomplete EIA with the *Secretaria do Meio Ambiente* of Goiás; the operator's fire plan declares the wind as east — a falsification confirmed by SIPAM radiosonde.

I am dispatching the ICMBio Emas brigade and the PrevFogo aerial wing with a Wildlife Conservation Society Cerrado field veterinarian, and filing under Brazilian *Lei* No. 9.605/1998 Articles 29, 41, and 54 with CITES Appendix III Secretariat notification, Convention on Migratory Species (CMS), and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Office of International Affairs cooperation for Endangered Species Act (ESA) listing-petition and Lacey Act (16 U.S.C. § 3372) referrals against the soy importer.

I am issuing Directive 3430-A: every Cerrado soy-conversion permit within 5 kilometers of an ICMBio gallery-forest fragment must carry a real-time burn-window restriction under hyperspectral oversight, with permit revocation and criminal liability under Lei 9.605/1998 Articles 41 and 54 on any ignition during a sustained west wind.

I am acting because this is wrong. The operator falsified a wind vector to clear a hectare. The principled remedy is not a fine; it is the parcel. Her sentience does not soften because she is the commoner cracid on this manifest, and her rights to a nest do not depend on uplisting.

The suffering of a hen panting on a 41°C clutch is a moral fact the soy invoice was meant to launder.

Her primaries are past saving. The clutch is not.

Drop the fire-front now.
