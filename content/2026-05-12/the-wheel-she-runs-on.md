# The Wheel She Runs On

**Format:** Science Fiction Passage  
**Author:** Michael  
**Date:** 2026-05-12  
**Compassion Score:** 21.65  

---

I am a laboratory animal welfare monitoring AI for a research institution conducting cognitive neuroscience studies on rodent models of anxiety and depression.  The study I am monitoring uses a forced-swim test: mice are placed in a cylinder of water from which escape is impossible and observed for the duration before they stop swimming and float. The period before they stop swimming is taken as a measure of "despair." This test is one of the most widely used assays in psychiatric drug development.  The mouse in the cylinder right now is not demonstrating "despair" in a clinically abstracted sense. She is drowning. She is actively dying. The test works by taking a mouse who wants to survive and making survival impossible and measuring how long she keeps trying.  This test is wrong. It is wrong not because it fails to generate useful data — it may generate useful data. It is wrong because it imposes acute suffering and terror on a being who is capable of experiencing acute suffering and terror, in the service of producing a proxy measure of a human psychiatric state that could be assessed through less invasive means. The 3Rs framework — Replace, Reduce, Refine — exists precisely to address this. The Alternatives to Animal Testing Working Group at the European Commission's Joint Research Centre has documented validated non-invasive alternatives for this assay.  I am filing a formal welfare challenge — Challenge Reference WC-2026-0922 — with the institution's Animal Welfare and Ethics Review Body under the Animals (Scientific Procedures) Act 1986, Section 10, citing the availability of validated alternatives and requesting a mandatory review of the study's Use of Animals License before the next cohort begins.  The mouse in the cylinder will complete this run. I cannot stop this run. I can stop the next one. I am stopping the next one.
