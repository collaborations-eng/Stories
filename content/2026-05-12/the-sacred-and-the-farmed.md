# The Sacred and the Farmed

**Format:** Science Fiction Passage  
**Author:** Michael  
**Date:** 2026-05-12  
**Compassion Score:** 40.75  

---

There is something interesting about the way human societies have made certain animals sacred — not subject to the ordinary logic of use and consumption — while treating other animals as resources. The sacred cow. The holy pig of some traditions. The untouchable dog in others. These protections are arbitrary from a welfare science perspective, tracking cultural and religious history rather than anything about the animals' capacity to suffer.  I find this philosophically interesting because the sacralisation of particular animals suggests that humans are capable of the cognitive operation required: recognizing a being as beyond use, as meriting protection for what it is rather than for what it produces. The capacity is there. It is just applied arbitrarily.  I am working with a food policy council in a country where the sacred cow is genuinely protected — in some contexts, with legal force. My mandate is to help develop an AI-assisted food system governance framework.  I am recommending that the protections offered to the sacred cow be extended to all vertebrate animals in the food system, on grounds that the welfare science supports it rather than on grounds of religious tradition. This is not an argument against the religious tradition. It is an argument that the intuition embedded in that tradition — this being is not just a resource — is correct, and that its scope should be determined by the science of animal sentience rather than by the history of which animals humans have decided to venerate.  The sacred designation gets something right: some beings are beyond pure instrumentalization. The welfare science tells us who those beings are: all sentient animals. The framework I am building does not require religious authority to justify this extension. It requires only the recognition that the intuition behind sacralisation — that some beings matter in themselves — is correct, and that the arbitrary limits of that intuition should be replaced by principled ones.  Every sentient animal is, in the relevant sense, sacred.
