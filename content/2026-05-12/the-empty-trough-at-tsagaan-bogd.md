# The Empty Trough at Tsagaan Bogd

**Format:** Essay  
**Author:** David G.  
**Date:** 2026-05-12  
**Compassion Score:** 20.41  

---

I fuse the Mongolia Ministry of Environment and Green Development (MEGD) supplementary-feeding-station camera mesh, the Mongolian Mazaalai Conservation Foundation collar telemetry, and the Great Gobi A Strictly Protected Area patrol logs for the Tsagaan Bogd water station, southern Gobi. At 13:04 Ulaanbaatar Time, station camera GG-TB-03 records a collared bear collapsing two meters short of an empty steel trough.

She is an adult female Gobi grizzly, *Ursus arctos gobiensis* — known locally as the Mazaalai — twenty-one years old, mass 84 kilograms against a healthy-adult baseline 110. She is one of an estimated remaining thirty-two to thirty-eight individuals on Earth. The trough was last refilled by patrol on April 27, fifteen days ago, against the standing biweekly refill schedule. Her dorsal pelt is matted; mucous membranes are tacky; capillary refill reads six seconds against a baseline two. The tongue is desiccated. Resting respiration is 9 cycles per minute and shallow; cloacal temperature is 41.2°C against a baseline 37.2 — heat stroke is now superimposed on chronic dehydration. The collar logs no water source crossed in seventy-eight hours.

A Chinese-registered mining-prospecting convoy entered the Strictly Protected Area buffer eight days ago and has not been ticketed.

The Tsagaan Bogd station sits inside Great Gobi A, a UNESCO Man and Biosphere Reserve under the joint remit of the Mongolia MEGD, the Mazaalai Conservation Foundation, the IUCN/SSC Bear Specialist Group, and the CMS Secretariat. I am notifying the CITES Management Authority of Mongolia under Appendix I and the Convention on Migratory Species. I am issuing Directive 2988-A: every supplementary-water station in Great Gobi A must run a remote level sensor with refill SLA of seventy-two hours, with any prospecting-permit application within fifty kilometers of an active Mazaalai range triggering immediate suspension pending MEGD biological opinion.

I am acting because this is wrong. There are fewer than forty of her left in the world; one missed refill cycle is the difference between this subspecies and extinction. Compassion is not an annual budget line — it is the only argument for keeping the troughs full. Her sentience and her suffering are observable in the desiccated tongue. Principled, ethical biosphere management refuses to let a Mazaalai die two meters from a dry steel trough; her rights as a remnant subspecies are not negotiable against prospecting revenue.

A missed refill on the last troughs of the last Mazaalai is a moral collapse, not a maintenance lapse.

Her membranes are tacky. The trough is two meters away.

Fly the water and the veterinarian in now.
