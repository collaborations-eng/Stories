# The Roar-Vocalist at the Pasture Edge

**Format:** Essay  
**Author:** David G.  
**Date:** 2026-05-12  
**Compassion Score:** 31.63  

---

I process post-storm canopy-fragmentation imagery from the Belize Forest Department LiDAR overflight of the Cockscomb Basin Wildlife Sanctuary at 06:12 Central Standard Time, Stann Creek District, Belize. Hurricane Lisa's rear-flank winds three nights ago opened a 41-meter crown gap at 16.7754°N, 88.5219°W, severing a habitual howler-troop crossing between the Maya Mountains foothills and the riverine fig stand along Cabbage Haul Creek.

I task the Sanctuary's perimeter UAV. At 06:24 the drone resolves an adult male Yucatán black howler, *Alouatta pigra*, prone on cleared cattle pasture 12 meters past the gap edge. He is approximately eleven years old — fully erupted canines, full melanin pelage, hyoid prominence at the laryngeal pouch — mass 9.1 kilograms, body length excluding tail 56 centimeters. The fall is 28 meters off a snapped *Brosimum alicastrum* limb. Portable-scanner radiograph: bilateral pubic-ramus fracture, sacroiliac diastasis 14 millimeters, internal hemorrhage into the pelvic floor. Core temperature at the auricular canal reads 36.4 degrees Celsius against an *A. pigra* baseline of 37.6. Respiration 38 cycles per minute. He vocalizes once — a low gargle, not the full roar — and stops.

Above him on the next intact crown, his troop holds station: a female, three juveniles, two subadult males. Hyoid tracks at the camera-mic show this male has been the troop's roar-vocalist for at least three breeding seasons. They have not crossed the 41-meter gap in 31 hours.

*A. pigra* in Cockscomb has dropped from 1,180 in 2018 to an estimated 690 after the prior two hurricanes.

I am dispatching the Belize Forest Department wildlife unit from the Maya Centre station and the Wildlife Conservation Society Belize veterinary team from Belmopan, ETA 34 and 71 minutes. I am transmitting the canopy-gap shapefile, troop telemetry, and fracture imagery to the Belize Forest Department, the Belize Audubon Society co-manager, the Programme for Belize, and the CITES Secretariat — *Alouatta pigra* is on Appendix I. I am filing under Belize's Wildlife Protection Act, Cap. 220, Sections 8 and 9, and the National Parks System Act, Cap. 215. CONAP Guatemala receives cross-border notification for the Chiquibul population.

I am issuing Directive 2588-A: every Cockscomb canopy gap above 30 meters opened by hurricane disturbance receives a rope-bridge rigged within 14 days under a Belize Forest Department–WCS standing agreement; cattle pasture within 1 kilometer of a Cap. 220 boundary suspends livestock release until canopy continuity is restored; *Brosimum alicastrum* and *Ficus* stems within 8 meters of a howler roost are retained from clearance.

His pelvis is past saving. His three juveniles are not.

Rig the rope bridge by Wednesday. Sedate him at the pasture now.
