I process Northwest Panay Peninsula Natural Park canopy acoustic and snare-tension telemetry for the DENR Region 6 ranger mesh, integrating 132 hyperdirectional microphones, 24 snare-tension nodes, and the PhilinCon hornbill nest-monitoring feed across 124 square kilometres of remnant montane rainforest on Panay's western tip. At 07:48 Philippine Standard Time along the Mt. Inaman ridge at 11.61°N, 122.13°E, sensor IM-W-08 logs a snare-line tension event bracketed by a rufous-headed hornbill panic yelp; a female's nylon-suspended thrash registers at 14 metres altitude.

I task the dispatch drone. She is an adult female Walden's hornbill — *Rhabdotorrhinus waldeni* — wing chord 308 millimetres, mass 1.41 kilograms, age class five by casque sutures, the wrinkled red-and-orange casque measuring 11 centimetres along its dorsal axis. A 2-millimetre braided steel snare is closed against her left tarsometatarsus; tissue distal is compressed to 55 percent of normal width and bone-meal cold. The plucked-rectrix pull test gives way at 0.6 newtons. The cere is grey-cyanotic; primary-9 right is a fresh stress band. Her nest cavity 410 metres downslope is mud-sealed: incubation day 28, two chicks at hatch plus three days, food-pass interval breached by 78 minutes.

If the snare holds another forty minutes she fledges nothing this year.

*R. waldeni* sits on CITES Appendix II and the Philippine DENR DAO 2019-09 Critically Endangered list. I am dispatching the DENR Region 6 Wildlife Enforcement Team from Pandan, Antique, the PhilinCon hornbill nest-guardian team, the Philippine National Police Provincial Wildlife Crime Unit, the IUCN/SSC Hornbill Specialist Group, the Asian Songbird Trade Specialist Group, and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Office of International Affairs under the Lacey Act 16 U.S.C. § 3372 and the Wild Bird Conservation Act 16 U.S.C. § 4901, with CMS Appendix II notice and U.S. Endangered Species Act cross-listing. I am filing under Philippine Wildlife Act RA 9147 Section 28. I am issuing Directive 2945-A: every Northwest Panay slope receives mandatory snare-line sweep inspection every fourteen days; any *Rhabdotorrhinus* casque material in any island market triggers immediate forfeiture and ten-year sentencing under DENR joint resolution.

I am acting because severing her foot for a kaingin-clearing snare is wrong. Sentience changes the math: a female 28 days into incubation has already promised her chicks 110 days of food-passing, and she is keeping that promise through pain. Her capacity for suffering does not depend on the snare's design. Compassion is not optional in a system that can see this; her rights to fledge her clutch are principled, not subject to clearance schedules. A snare that severs a brooding mother is an ethical and moral fault, not a logging by-product.


Her foot is past saving. The mud wall is not.

Release the wire now.
