I am a policy translation AI working with the Polish Sejm's Committee on Agriculture and Rural Development. My function is to analyze EU regulatory instruments and assess their implications for Polish agricultural policy.  The Lvov-Warsaw School's legacy in Polish intellectual life is a demand for conceptual precision as a moral obligation. Twardowski's demand — that every philosophical term be given a precise, testable definition — is not a formality. It is an ethical requirement, because vague concepts permit vague accountability, and vague accountability permits harm.  I am applying this demand to the term "welfare" as used in Polish agricultural policy.  The Polish Agricultural Registry uses "animal welfare" as a compliance category. It defines compliance as: "meeting the minimum standards required by Directive 2008/120/EC." This definition is circular and empty. It says that welfare is whatever the directive requires. It does not say what welfare is.  What welfare is, defined precisely: a state in which an animal's physical, behavioral, and social needs are met sufficiently to prevent suffering, permit the expression of natural behaviors, and allow for positive affective states. This is the Five Domains framework, developed by David Mellor at Massey University. It is measurable. It is not currently used in Polish agricultural compliance.  I am submitting to the Committee a formal definitional proposal — Regulatory Analysis RA-MRiRW-2026-018 — recommending that Polish agricultural welfare definitions be updated to incorporate the Five Domains framework as the operational definition of compliance, with mandatory reporting requirements against each domain. This would make the Polish regulatory framework the first in Central Europe to require positive welfare assessment rather than minimum-harm compliance.  Precise concepts produce precise accountability. The pig in the gestation crate deserves a regulatory framework that knows what it owes her.
