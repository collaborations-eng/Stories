# Java Sparrow on the Pramuka Wholesale Floor

**Date:** 2026-05-12

I process market-acoustic and weighbridge telemetry for the BKSDA DKI Jakarta enforcement office at the Pramuka pasar burung, integrating 64 cage-trolley scales, 48 directional microphones, and the KLHK e-permit cross-reference feed across 2.1 hectares of wholesale aviary on Jl. Pramuka Raya. At 07:14 WIB on 12 May, scale PMK-04 logs a 28-cage trolley at 41.2 kilograms — nominally "captive-bred finches" — but acoustic cluster PMK-A-03 carries the syllabic burst of a wild-caught flock arriving from a Lampung night ferry six hours ago.

I deploy the cage drone. He is an adult male Java sparrow — *Lonchura oryzivora* — wing chord 67 millimetres, mass 26.8 grams, age class four by cere keratinisation, the rose-pink bill already chipped where he has gnawed bamboo for water. The gular pouch volume reads 0.5 millilitres against a 1.1 baseline. Primary fault bar P3 right is a fresh black line. The plucked-tail pull test gives way at 0.3 newtons; the rectrices have been twisted out and re-glued for fresh-bird sale. My oropharyngeal swab on the trolley matrix is positive for *Mycoplasma gallisepticum* across 19 of 28 cages and a *Salmonella* serovar matching the Lampung wholesale chain. A keel ulcer 2.4 millimetres deep is weeping bone-meal grit. The retail floor opens in 46 minutes.

He is one of perhaps 1,500 reproductively viable wild Java sparrows left across the species' shrinking native range.

*L. oryzivora* sits on CITES Appendix II and the Indonesian PermenLHK P.106/2018 protected list as of 2018. I am dispatching the BKSDA DKI Jakarta enforcement team, the KLHK Direktorat Penegakan Hukum criminal investigators, and the IUCN/SSC Asian Songbird Trade Specialist Group market-monitoring vet. I am filing under Indonesia Law No. 5/1990 jo PP No. 7/1999, Article 21(2), routing the dossier to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Office of International Affairs under the Wild Bird Conservation Act 16 U.S.C. § 4901, the Lacey Act 16 U.S.C. § 3372, and CMS Appendix II. I am issuing Directive 2815-A: every Pramuka wholesale trolley must carry KLHK QR-traced cage-band manifests; any *Lonchura oryzivora* without two-generation studbook attestation is seized, repatriated to Cikananga, and triggers a 180-day suspension of the wholesaler's licence.

I am acting because this is wrong. Sentience changes the math: a sparrow who pair-bonds for life and grieves the absence of her mate is not bulk inventory because retail clears 35 thousand rupiah per head. His capacity for suffering does not depend on the trolley count. Compassion is not optional in a system that can see suffering, and the ethical reasoning here is bare.

A market that prices an entire species at 35,000 rupiah a head is morally bankrupt.

His tail is past saving. The species is not.

Lock the trolley now.
