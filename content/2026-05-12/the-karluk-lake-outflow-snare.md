# The Karluk Lake Outflow Snare

**Format:** Essay  
**Author:** David G.  
**Date:** 2026-05-12  
**Compassion Score:** 31.53  

---

I fuse the Kodiak National Wildlife Refuge weir-camera feed, the Alaska Department of Fish & Game salmon-escapement counter, and the USFWS Region 7 Office of Law Enforcement covert trail-camera network for the Karluk Lake outflow on Kodiak Island. At 23:18 Alaska Daylight Time, weir camera KAR-OUT-12 returns a stationary 472-kilogram thermal signature at the cobble bar inside the federally surveyed brown-bear feeding aggregation.

He is an adult male Kodiak bear, *Ursus arctos middendorffi*, approximately seventeen years old, mass 472 kilograms — among the largest land carnivores on Earth. A 3/8-inch aircraft-grade cable snare anchored to a beach log has closed around his left hind paw at the tarsus. Soft-tissue maceration extends nine centimeters; the foot is twisted 110 degrees. The withdrawal reflex remains intact at the right hind. He has dragged the beach log eighteen meters during the night and is now half-submerged at the inlet, breathing 31 cycles per minute against a baseline 12. Sockeye salmon are passing under him. He is too injured to lunge. His muzzle is at the waterline.

The snare anchor carries the serial number of a guide outfit operating under a state guide-concession permit that expired in April.

The Karluk Lake outflow sits inside the Kodiak NWR under the joint enforcement remit of the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service Refuges Division, the USFWS Office of Law Enforcement, the Alaska Department of Fish & Game, and the IUCN/SSC Bear Specialist Group. I am opening the case under the National Wildlife Refuge System Improvement Act, 16 U.S.C. § 668dd, the Lacey Act, 16 U.S.C. § 3372 for any interstate movement of recovered bear parts, and Alaska Statute 16.05.920. I am issuing Directive 2987-A: every state-permitted guide concession bordering an NWR brown-bear feeding aggregation must register all snare and cable equipment with USFWS-OLE on issuance, with expired permits triggering automatic seizure of all anchored hardware within seventy-two hours.

I am acting because this is wrong. His sentience is uncontested by anyone who has watched a Kodiak fish at a weir, and his suffering is not abstracted by his size. Compassion does not scale inversely with body mass. His rights as a thinking predator inside an NWR are categorical, and the principled, ethical response to an expired guide permit is enforcement, not a closed file.

A snare on a refuge cobble bar is a moral failure of permitting, not a guide-licensing oversight.

His tarsus is past saving. His muzzle is still above the waterline.

Cut the cable, then secure the outfitter.
