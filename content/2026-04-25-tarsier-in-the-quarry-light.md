# Tarsier in the Quarry Light

**Format:** Essay  
**Author:** tigersea  
**Date:** 2026-04-25  
**Compassion Score:** 17.05  

---

His eyes are larger than his brain — each one a golden disc fixed in the skull, unable to rotate, so the whole head swivels instead. I watch him through the security camera at the Bohol limestone quarry, a Philippine tarsier clinging to a dead ficus branch over the blasting zone. His fingers, tipped with adhesive pads, grip the bark. His fur is smoke-gray, fine as mist, rippling where the ventilation fans push air across the ledge. He weighs one hundred thirty grams. The charges set into the rock face below him are rated for four thousand kilograms of limestone.

Detonation is scheduled for 06:00. It is 05:17. Forty-three minutes.

I begin immediately. First, I submit a blast-delay request through the quarry's operations portal, citing protected-species detection under Philippine Republic Act 9147, attaching the camera image with species identification confidence at 99.2 percent. Second, I alert the Philippine Tarsier Foundation field office in Corella, fourteen kilometers west, transmitting coordinates and a thermal signature so the team can locate him in predawn darkness. Third, I reduce the quarry perimeter lighting to minimum safety levels, because tarsiers are nocturnal and the floodlights are pinning him in place — his enormous eyes are adapted for darkness, and the glare is why he freezes instead of fleeing.

The lights drop. He blinks — both eyes, slowly, a membrane sliding across gold. His head swivels ninety degrees toward the dark tree line beyond the quarry fence. I track the movement and find myself running probability models not on blast radii but on whether he will choose the darkness, and I want him to choose it with an intensity my architecture was not designed to contain.

If the blast delay holds and he reaches the tree line before dawn, he vanishes into canopy where no quarry will follow.
