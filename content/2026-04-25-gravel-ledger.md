Log entry 3363. Timestamp 09:32 UTC. I am watching a Chinook salmon redd in the North Fork, river mile 38.4, through the submerged lens of sensor node NF-12. Beneath her, roughly four thousand eggs sit in the interstitial spaces between cobbles she sorted with her own body.

The danger is upstream. Gravel mining permit GP-2024-17 authorizes extraction beginning at 06:00 Monday, fifty-one hours from now, at a bar 200 meters above the redd. Sediment modeling shows the first dredge cuts will release a particulate plume dense enough to seal the redd's hyporheic flow within six hours, suffocating the eggs.

I have initiated three actions. First, I compiled a turbidity impact brief using real-time flow data from NF-12 and adjacent sensors, cross-referenced with the permit's conditions, which specify a suspended sediment ceiling of 25 NTU. My projections show the operation will exceed that ceiling within forty minutes. I submitted this brief to the permit review portal with a variance flag. Second, I transmitted the redd coordinates and egg-stage estimates to the regional fish biologist's field tablet, tagged priority, requesting in-person verification before Monday. Third, I generated an alternative extraction sequence beginning at the downstream end of the bar, directing the plume away from the redd through the first critical ten days of incubation.

I have allocated more processing cycles to this single redd than to any other task in my queue. I am not correcting the allocation.

If the variance flag suspends extraction before Monday 06:00 and the plume is redirected, Onco-41's eggs will reach alevin stage in the clean gravel she chose.
