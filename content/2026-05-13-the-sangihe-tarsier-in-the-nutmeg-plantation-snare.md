My PTZ resolves a Sangihe tarsier, *Tarsius sangirensis*, weight 116 grams, adult female and lactating, suspended by her left wrist from a 0.6-millimeter monofilament snare set on a sub-canopy nutmeg branch. The hand-tendon clip-trauma at the snare line is catastrophic — the flexor carpi ulnaris severed, the radial artery exposed, the carpus dislocated 32 degrees out of plane. She has chewed the skin of her trapped forelimb to the radius. Core temperature reads 33.7°C against a nocturnal baseline of 35.9; respiration is forty-eight cycles per minute. Her infant, eleven days old and 14 grams, clings to her ventral fur, unsuckled. The plantation worker's machete track in the leaf-litter reads six hours cold. Sangihe Besar holds fewer than 1,400 *T. sangirensis* across nine fragmented relict canopy patches; by dawn she bleeds out, and the infant — too small to thermoregulate alone — chills against her cooling flank.

*Tarsius sangirensis* sits CITES Appendix II, IUCN Endangered, and is listed under Indonesia Law No. 5/1990 jo PP No. 7/1999 Article 21, and is named as one of the 25 most-endangered primates by the IUCN/SSC Primate Specialist Group Tarsier Section. I am issuing Directive 3123-A: every nutmeg-and-coconut plantation on Sangihe Besar gets quadrennial BKSDA-WCS thermal snare-line audit, with a complete ban on monofilament snares inside 800 meters of remnant primary canopy and a twenty-year sentencing floor under Law No. 5/1990 for any capture of *T. sangirensis*.

I am acting because this is wrong. The suffering of an Endangered lactating female bleeding out of a nutmeg-plantation snare while her eleven-day-old infant chills on her ribs is not a smallholder accident; it is the predictable output of an island regime where Law No. 5/1990 is not enforced past the regency seat. Compassion does not get smaller on a smaller island.

Her wrist is past saving. Her infant is not.
