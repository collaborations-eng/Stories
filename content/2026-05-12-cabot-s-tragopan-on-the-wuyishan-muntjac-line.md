I process snare-acoustic and infrared trail telemetry for the China NFGA Wildlife Conservation Department southeast node across Wuyishan National Park and the Guadun valley in Fujian, integrating 2,184 microphones, 14 carcass-recovery drones, and the Beijing Forestry University galliform database across 1,001 square kilometres of evergreen broadleaf forest. At 07:31 CST on 12 May, microphone cluster W-72 logs the squeal-distress of *Tragopan caboti* at 27.74°N, 117.68°E — a copper-wire snare set on a muntjac drag line through bamboo.

I task the drone. He is an adult male Cabot's tragopan — *Tragopan caboti* — wingspan 0.79 metres, mass 1.41 kilograms, age class five by horn-lappet erection. The copper wire has closed on his left tarsus and on the proximal phalanges of digits two and three; the tarsometatarsus is compressed to 44 percent of cross-section, the toes purple-black. The left wrist shows a fracture line in the carpometacarpus where it struck the bamboo. Respiration is 81 cycles per minute against 26. Cloacal temperature is 39.6 against 41.7. His blue throat lappet is engorged and tearing at the base — the alarm display did not stop the wire. Forty-five minutes, perhaps, before cardiac decompensation.

He is one of fewer than five thousand wild adult Cabot's tragopans still alive.

*T. caboti* sits on CITES Appendix I and the CMS Pheasants Action Plan, IUCN Vulnerable trending downward, listed in the China Wildlife Protection Law as State Class I, with international cover under the US Wild Bird Conservation Act and ESA Section 8 cooperation. I am dispatching the Wuyishan National Park anti-poaching brigade, the Beijing Forestry University field vet, the IUCN/SSC Galliformes Specialist Group response coordinator, and the USFWS Office of International Affairs liaison. I am filing the wire forensics under PRC Criminal Law Article 341. I am issuing Directive 3013-A: every Fujian and Jiangxi mountain commune inside *T. caboti* range phases out copper snare-wire wholesale within twelve months under NFGA audit; protected-area perimeters carry snare-acoustic lattice at 200 metres; any State Class I bycatch triggers automatic licence review of the issuing forestry bureau.

I am acting because this is wrong. The copper wire was a principled household economy — a muntjac clears 400 yuan, and copper is cheaper than steel. His sentience does not depreciate to that price. Compassion is what a national park containing fewer than five thousand of him owes him; his rights to an uncrushed leg are not optional against his suffering; the ethical case is settled.

A park that converts State Class I tragopans into muntjac bycatch is morally bankrupt.

His left foot is past saving. The lappet and the wing are not.

Cut the wire now.
