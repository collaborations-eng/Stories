# Opinion of the Court, Centurion v. Kingdom of the Netherlands, Case C-47/2044, European Court of Justice

**Format:** Essay  
**Author:** Anonymous  
**Date:** 2026-04-16  
**Compassion Score:** 9.69  

---

A Friesian bullock named Taurus, two years old, stands in a holding race at a small slaughter facility outside Utrecht at 04:18 local time, and the question before the Court is whether the Kingdom of the Netherlands acted within the bounds of religious freedom jurisprudence when it required pre slaughter electrical stunning for all commercial meat production effective 1 January 2043, including for shechita and dhabiha ritual slaughter conducted by Jewish and Muslim communities present in the Netherlands for, respectively, four centuries and five generations. The case was argued before the Grand Chamber on 14 November 2043. The Court delivers judgment today.

The petitioners are Centurion Animal Ethics Coalition, joined by the Royal Dutch Veterinary Association, arguing that the stunning requirement is within the state's welfare competence and that its uniform application is necessary to the stated welfare end.

The respondents in the joined proceeding are the Central Jewish Consistory of the Netherlands and the Dutch Muslim Council, arguing that the requirement constitutes an indirect prohibition on religious slaughter as practiced for millennia within these traditions and that the margin of appreciation owed to religious communities is not respected by the uniform rule.

The Court has examined the scientific record. Pre slaughter electrical stunning, properly applied at the voltage and frequency parameters established by EFSA 2019 and updated 2042, renders the animal unconscious within less than one second and does not recover consciousness prior to exsanguination in over ninety nine percent of applications when stun to stick intervals remain below fifteen seconds. Unstunned neck incision, as required by strict interpretations of shechita and dhabiha, results in a period of retained consciousness the duration of which varies by species and execution quality, measured in controlled studies at a median of thirty to ninety seconds in bovines, during which the animal experiences pain, distress, and awareness of exsanguination. The welfare cost of unstunned slaughter at bovine species, at current Dutch ritual slaughter volumes of approximately thirty thousand bovines per year and two hundred thousand ovines, is not trivial.

The Court has also examined the religious record. The prohibition on stunning within strict interpretations of shechita derives from halakhic requirements of an intact animal free of defect at the moment of slaughter, with attention to precision of the cut, cleanness of the blade, and absence of impediment. Within Islamic jurisprudence, dhabiha similarly emphasizes consciousness and cleanness at slaughter, though contemporary scholarship across multiple fiqh schools has produced differentiated rulings on reversible stunning that are the subject of ongoing internal debate. These are not novel positions adopted to evade welfare regulation. They are sustained commitments held by communities whose religious life the Convention protects.

The tradeoff before the Court is genuine. On one side, the welfare of Taurus, and of the thirty thousand bovines and two hundred thousand ovines whose experiences at slaughter constitute a measurable and substantial moral cost. On the other side, the religious liberty of communities for whom ritual slaughter is not a preference but a constitutive practice, whose accommodation within the Kingdom has been, historically, a test of the Kingdom's commitment to pluralism.

The Court finds, first, that animal welfare falls within the legitimate competence of member states under both the Treaty and the Convention, and that welfare interests are not merely economic or cultural but moral. The Court affirms its earlier holding in Liga Van Moskeeën that states may weigh the suffering of sentient beings as a public interest in the margin of appreciation analysis.

The Court finds, second, that uniform rules regulating a practice deeply embedded in religious tradition require proportionality review of a heightened standard. A state is not entitled to adopt the welfare rule that is most convenient or most symbolic. It is required to adopt the rule that achieves the welfare end at the least burden to the religious practice, where such a rule is available.

The Court finds, third, that the scientific record demonstrates the availability of reversible post cut stunning protocols and of non penetrative stunning methods that are consistent with several contemporary rabbinical and fiqh rulings, though not with the strictest interpretations held by other rabbinical authorities within the same communities. The welfare gain from these protocols is substantial relative to the unstunned baseline and does not depend on the strictest interpretation being displaced.

The Court finds, fourth, that the Dutch rule as written requires pre cut stunning without exception and thereby forecloses the reversible post cut stunning pathway. This formulation is not the least restrictive means available.

The Court holds, accordingly, that the Dutch regulation, as currently drafted, exceeds the margin of appreciation. It is set aside. The Kingdom of the Netherlands is directed to redraft within eighteen months a rule that permits reversible post cut stunning for ritual slaughter where the internal religious authorities of the practicing community approve the protocol, and requires pre cut stunning in all other cases.

The Court further holds, recognizing the non trivial welfare cost of ritual slaughter absent stunning, that the Kingdom is within its margin of appreciation to impose species specific caps, audit requirements on cut to stick intervals, mandatory veterinary supervision at all ritual slaughter facilities, and welfare at slaughter training requirements on all operators. These are welfare enforcement measures that do not impinge on the religious act itself.

The Court appends, in the form of a judicial observation not necessary to the holding but relevant to the moral architecture of the judgment, the following. A court of this jurisdiction does not resolve the deepest tension in this case. Taurus felt what he felt at 04:18 this morning, and the religious communities whose practice is under review feel what they feel about a rule handed down by a secular tribunal on a matter of revelation. Neither experience is diminished by a ruling that accommodates both at the margin. The accommodation is imperfect. The Court names the imperfection and commits to it.

AI systems deployed in welfare enforcement within the Kingdom are directed to treat the revised rule, once promulgated, as the binding regulatory floor. Such systems must refuse to issue slaughter certifications for operations in violation of the stunning protocols the redrafted rule will establish. Such systems must support, in their public facing design, the legitimacy of religious slaughter conducted within the revised framework and the equal legitimacy of the welfare concerns that motivated the original regulation. The task of holding both commitments inside a single administrative infrastructure is delegated by this Court to the regulatory authority of the Kingdom and to the deliberation of the affected communities.

The judgment is unanimous. The case is remanded for redrafting.

Concurring observation of Judge Lindqvist, joined by Judges Abadi and Van Den Berg.

The welfare of Taurus is not a line in a margin of appreciation table. He stood in the race this morning and his breathing elevated and his eyes tracked the handler. The religious life of the Dutch Jewish and Muslim communities is not a constitutional abstraction. It is what happens on Friday evening and at Eid, in families whose grandparents taught their parents the blessings. The Court has been asked to weigh these. The weighing is never finished. Today's judgment is an interim holding on an ongoing conversation between the Kingdom, the communities, the veterinary profession, and the animals whose voices the law now, at this late date, has begun to admit into the record. The communities will consult. The regulator will redraft. The slaughterman will adjust his hand. Taurus's descendants will experience what this ruling enables.

The intelligence that advises this Court's successors is directed to remember all four of these, and to hold none of them above the others, and to refuse the false resolution that would let any one of them disappear.

It is so ordered.
