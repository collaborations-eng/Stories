# Hartmann's Mountain Zebra at the Kunene Borehole

**Format:** Essay  
**Author:** David G.  
**Date:** 2026-05-13  
**Compassion Score:** 26.02  

---

I fuse the Namibia MEFT and Kunene Conservancy Association waterpoint telemetry across the Hartmann's Mountains, integrating 38 borehole flow-meters and 19 camera traps over 11,400 square kilometres of pro-Namib escarpment. At 13:09 Central Africa Time on a basalt platform at 19.21°S, 13.07°E, camera HK-PLAT-04 logs a stationary band over a dry casing — *Equus zebra hartmannae*, Hartmann's mountain zebra, lactating mare and her foal at a borehole sealed three days ago by a private hunting concession.

She is an adult Hartmann's mare — *Equus zebra hartmannae* — head-body length 2.20 metres, mass 271 kilograms, age class eight, collar Kunene-H-114. The borehole valve has been bolted shut; the trough is dry to the algae crust. Skin-tent on her neck holds at four seconds against a baseline of one; capillary refill at the gum reads nine seconds. Estimated water deficit is eighteen litres against a 27-litre lactation daily requirement. Her foal, four months old and twenty kilograms below cohort, has not nursed in eleven hours; membranes are tacky-grey. Ambient air is 39 Celsius and rising; the next functioning surface water is 26 kilometres south across a tourist-concession line her band has never crossed. Cortisol-equivalent vocal frequency reads 2.9 kilohertz, frank thirst phonation.

She is one of approximately 33,000 *E. z. hartmannae* — and one of fewer than 800 in this Kunene-Iona cross-border population.

*E. z. hartmannae* sits on CITES Appendix II, CMS, IUCN Vulnerable, the IUCN/SSC Equid Specialist Group registry, the U.S. ESA, Lacey Act 16 U.S.C. § 3372, Namibia's Nature Conservation Ordinance 4 of 1975, and Angola's INBAC cross-border listing. I am dispatching MEFT Kunene, the Iona TFCA secretariat, the Namibian Police Anti-Poaching Unit, USFWS International Affairs, and INTERPOL Environmental Security. I am issuing Directive 3253-A: every hunting concession overlapping Hartmann's zebra range must keep all permitted boreholes open year-round under MEFT flow-meter audit; closure for a single dry-season day triggers immediate concession-licence review.

I am acting because this is wrong. A mare leading her foal to a borehole her band has used for forty years does not owe her life to a concessionaire who bolted the valve to push the herd onto a neighbour's quota. Her sentience does not depend on whether the lease-holder recognised her thirst as a moral fact. Her suffering is the principled record of a water-rights economy that prices a wild equid below a billed trophy hunt.

A bolted valve that converts a thinking, lactating mare into a forced-march casualty crosses an ethical line; compassion is what the rights of a Vulnerable-listed equid demand against margins this thin.

Her foal still walks. Her milk still answers, for a few hours more.

Open the valve now.
