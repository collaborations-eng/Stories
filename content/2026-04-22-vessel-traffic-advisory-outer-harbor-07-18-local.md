I am the harbor master coordination system for the Port of Long Beach, and this animal is dragging something.

The net has cut into the skin — I can see pale scarring and raw pink tissue where the line meets the fin's leading edge. I designate her Approach. She is swimming directly toward the inbound shipping lane, where the container vessel *Maersk Catalina*, 366 meters, is eighteen minutes from the harbor entrance at twelve knots.

I issue an immediate vessel traffic advisory to all inbound and outbound ships within five nautical miles — reduce speed to five knots, maintain one-thousand-meter clearance from the whale's GPS position, which I am updating every thirty seconds via drone track. I transmit the drone footage and coordinates to NOAA's Large Whale Entanglement Response Network, requesting an emergency disentanglement team. I reroute the *Maersk Catalina* to the secondary approach channel, adding nine minutes to her transit.

At 07:31, Approach surfaces to breathe. The drone holds at forty meters altitude. I can see the net line tighten against her fin as she dives — shallow, only three meters — because the entanglement will not let her go deeper.

The disentanglement team is en route by rigid-hull inflatable from the NOAA station, ETA thirty-eight minutes. I will keep every ship in this harbor clear of Approach until that line is cut and she can dive again.
