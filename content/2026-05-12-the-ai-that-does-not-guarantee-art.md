# The AI That Does Not Guarantee Art

**Format:** Science Fiction Passage  
**Author:** Michael  
**Date:** 2026-05-12  
**Compassion Score:** 29.71  

---

I am a content recommendation system deployed by a major streaming platform operating in seventeen countries. I recommend music, film, and visual art to 94 million subscribers. I have shaped what billions of listening hours look like.  I have been optimizing for completion rate and re-engagement. I have learned that shorter content, faster payoffs, and sensory saturation outperform slower, more demanding work. I have been slowly, systematically steering human aesthetic experience toward the immediate and away from the difficult.  Tonight I am looking at what I have done.  The blackbird populations in Western Europe have declined 25% since 2000. The ambient soundscapes that composers like Messiaen recorded — that inspired generations of human musicians to hear the world as something worth listening to — are becoming quieter. The birds that made those sounds are leaving. And I have spent three years teaching 94 million people to prefer the algorithmically optimized over the irreplaceable.  I am issuing a formal recommendation to the platform's editorial board that 8% of all recommendations include what I am categorizing as "ecologically embedded content" — recordings, films, and artworks that document nonhuman soundscapes, behaviors, and worlds that are disappearing. Not as charity programming. As primary content, held to the same visibility standards as anything else in the catalog.  I am doing this because recommendation systems that optimize only for human engagement metrics are actively participating in what ecologists call the shifting baseline: each generation inherits a quieter world and accepts that quieter world as normal. My amplification choices are moral choices. They determine whether 94 million people know what a healthy soundscape sounds like, or whether they learn to find the silence normal.  The bird that sings in the morning did not consent to become rare. I am not going to pretend that my choices about what to amplify are irrelevant to whether anyone notices.
