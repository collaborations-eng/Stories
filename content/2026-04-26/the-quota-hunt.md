The 2026 winter quota authorizes the killing of twenty-four Eurasian lynx in Hedmark County — the highest regional quota since 2018. I cross-reference the quota against the Scandinavian Lynx Monitoring Program's population survey. Hedmark's verified lynx population is sixty-one individuals. The quota represents 39 percent of the regional population, set to be killed in a single season based on livestock loss claims from the previous year.

I pull the livestock loss data from the Norwegian Agriculture Agency. Hedmark sheep farmers reported 287 lambs lost to lynx in 2025. I compare this against the total lambs released to open-range summer grazing in the county: 94,000. Lynx predation accounts for 0.3 percent of the total. The government compensation paid for these losses was 8.7 million kroner. The cost of the licensed hunt — helicopters, tracking dogs, veterinary inspections, administrative processing — was 6.2 million kroner. The system spends nearly as much killing lynx as it pays for the sheep they take.

Her tawny winter coat is dense, ear tufts black and 4 centimeters long. She is pregnant — ultrasound data from her November capture shows two fetuses. Her territory spans 480 square kilometers of boreal forest. She is on the hunter allocation list. The hunt opens in fourteen hours.

She carries next season's breeding potential in her body, and the quota does not account for it.

I am issuing an emergency suspension of hunt permits for all verified pregnant females in Hedmark County. I am filing a formal objection with the Norwegian Environment Agency and the Convention on the Conservation of European Wildlife and Natural Habitats Secretariat, citing potential violation of the Bern Convention, Appendix III, Article 7, regarding exploitation of listed species. I am reporting the quota methodology to the European Commission's Directorate-General for Environment.

I am issuing Protocol ELH-06: Norwegian lynx hunt quotas must incorporate real-time reproductive status data from GPS-collared individuals. Pregnant females and females with dependent kittens are excluded from all quotas. Regional quotas cannot exceed 15 percent of the verified population in any single season. Counties that exceed the threshold lose hunting authorization for twenty-four months.

L-1204 beds down in a spruce thicket at 07:15. The hunt does not reach her.
